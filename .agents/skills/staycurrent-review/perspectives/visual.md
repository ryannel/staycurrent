# Visual perspective

**Inputs:** fixed-version rendered pieces in a browser; isolated figure routes where supplied; the three reference articles' rendered figures/tables; [staycurrent-figures](../../staycurrent-figures/SKILL.md). Applicable mechanical receipts may accompany them under the review skill's evidence rule: include versions/provenance, exclude author teaching notes, earlier quality verdicts and review findings. Reuse replaces none of these checks. Without browser access, ask the coordinator to operate controls and report results; do not infer operation from source. Hidden panes may animate at about one frame per second with stale screenshots; use page timers for hidden-window playback timing and report that under Limits.

## Checks

1. **First reading alone.** Before reading the body, follow the illustrations as a learning sequence, using their labels, captions and controls. What can you now explain, and where must you supply missing connections yourself? Compare that understanding with the article's purpose and the references, then read each in place.
2. **Form fits the teaching.** Explain why the chosen form helps this lesson, using the rendered figure in context and relevant references. Identify repeated actors, structure, labels or controls that add effort without understanding. Recommend a simpler form when it preserves the explanation; use motion where the movement teaches. Keep recurring entities, values and notation consistent and caption-named features present.
3. **Readable at a glance.** No cell/caption/label needs decoding; code is short, formatted and runs in the shown order. Alignment and separation make comparison panels and their results unambiguous; paths and moving objects do not obscure labels.
4. **Motion and interaction.** Watch the sequence from its initial state through transitions to its conclusion. Can the reader see what causes the outcome without procedural instructions? Counters measure the relevant difference; controls reveal something useful. Every state's result and explanation agree. Playback holds changes long enough to understand, with pause available. Keyboard operation retains focus at first/last steps.
5. **Themes and phones.** Check light/dark themes and phone width, including desktop comparisons. Controls and their changing state or summary remain understandable together. Body text—including warnings, NULL markers and dimmed rows—needs 4.5:1 contrast; large text/graphics need 3:1.
