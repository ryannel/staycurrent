# Reader and visual lens

Read as the intelligent newcomer the article is for, then look at the figures as a
designer would.

**Inputs:** the finished article rendered in a browser, the audience,
[Figure style: ink and tokens](../../../../docs/figure-style.md), and the
[voice guide](../../staycurrent-write/references/voice.md). On a first reading, do not
read plans, notes or earlier verdicts.

## The prose

1. **Openings.** Each opening gives enough context to understand the question and why it
   matters.
2. **Plain, varied sentences.** The prose needs no decoding, and its rhythm varies rather
   than marching in uniform short sentences.
3. **Terms.** Each term is explained at first use and used consistently. Headings use the
   field's own terms.
4. **Confidence.** Flag repeated disclaimers, defensive negation, recaps of running-example
   facts that teach nothing here, and deferrals ("X owns that") where the reader needed
   an answer. Name the passages, and estimate how often each occurs.
5. **One piece.** The article reads as if written in one go, with no patch-like asides or
   stacked qualifications.
6. **Prose stands alone.** Read the whole article with every figure, caption and control
   omitted. Report missing premises, unexplained results and passages that narrate a
   figure. Removing figure references alone does not establish this.

## The figures

7. **Contribution.** Name what each figure makes easier to understand. Suggest a simpler
   form, or no figure, where the figure adds effort without understanding.
8. **House style.** Check each figure against the defect list in the figure style:
   overlaps, labels on tokens, token treatment, values drawn where they matter, one title.
9. **Motion and controls.** Watch each figure from its first frame to its settled final
   frame. The cause of the outcome is visible without instructions, and the controls work
   by keyboard.
10. **Themes and phones.** Check light and dark, desktop and 375 px. Text contrast is
    4.5:1, and large text and graphics 3:1.

Without browser access, complete the prose checks and report the rendered checks as not
done. Hidden panes can throttle animation, so bring the figure into view before judging
it.
