# First visual reading

**Inputs:** the fixed figures-only preview, the article's title and intended audience,
and these instructions. Start without the article body, source code, intended lessons,
exit questions, teaching plan, author notes or earlier reviews. Tell the coordinator if
you have already seen them or if the preview exposes them; that reading cannot serve
as independent evidence. Do not seek them yourself.

Use the visible figures, labels, captions and controls. Read each figure as an
explanation in its own right, then follow the sequence. Watch animations from their
initial state through the changes to the settled result; use the offered variants.
Long text alternatives are checked later, not used to explain the drawing during this
visual test. Do not infer motion or meaning from implementation code.

Record what you understand as you go, before later explanations change your account:

- What is the setup, what happens, why, and what can you conclude or decide? Point to
  the visible evidence for that account. Distinguish what was shown from what you
  supplied from prior knowledge or another figure.
- Where did you have to guess, reread a caption or replay? Say which connection or
  distinction was unclear. Note useful motion and controls as well as confusing ones.
- After the sequence, explain the subject as far as these visuals taught it. What can
  you now predict or choose, and what questions remain? Do not invent missing lessons.

State the build, viewport, variants watched and whether you observed transitions or
only stills. Bring the animation into view before judging it; hidden panes may throttle
playback. Without browser access, return the limitation rather than a comprehension pass.

Save and return this account unchanged before receiving the learning goals, full article
or [informed visual checks](visual.md). Keep observations separate from proposed repairs.
