# Senior practitioner lens

Read as an engineer who runs this technology in production and has to approve the article
for new hires.

**Inputs:** the article or articles; the dossier; the exit questions; the parent, sibling
and child briefs; the sources and any execution evidence; the commission.

## Checks

1. **Correctness.** Check consequential claims against primary sources such as official
   documentation, specifications or source code, and name the sources you checked.
   Disposable local reproduction is welcome; record the version and commands used.
2. **Observed or illustrative.** Illustrations are labelled as such, and observed results
   match the recorded evidence. No unrun command is presented as observed.
3. **Expert completeness.** Every dossier item this article owns is taught at the level
   an expert uses it: names, magnitudes, output, code, failure patterns. Name what is
   missing, and why it matters to a decision.
4. **Depth in both directions.** Name missing understanding and unnecessary depth.
5. **Best alternative.** Name the best existing explanation of this subject that you
   know of. Say what it has that this article lacks, and whether you would send a new
   hire here instead.
6. **Ownership.** The article gives a useful answer at its level and leaves deeper
   mechanisms to their owning child, without duplicating a full treatment.

For a plan or collection, also check that every needed subject has exactly one owner.
For a plan, check that its proposed visual coverage includes the main mechanisms,
consequential failures and practical choices; detailed implementation may stay in prose.
Count the pages a typical task needs, and name any merges that would save openings
without losing understanding. For each proposed cut or merge, state the reader effort
saved and the understanding lost.
