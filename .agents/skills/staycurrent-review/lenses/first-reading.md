# First reading

**Inputs:** prose extracted from the finished rendering, or the supplied prose for a
prose-only commission; the intended audience; these instructions. Start without the author's
conversation, plan, exit questions, writing guide or previous verdicts. Tell the
coordinator if you have already seen those inputs.

Read as a curious person encountering the subject. Record observations as they happen,
before later passages or author explanations can change your account.

## Follow the prose

First read with all figures, captions, labels and controls omitted. The prose must
explain the subject completely on its own.

- Before each section, note what its heading leads you to expect; afterwards, compare.
- At the end of the opening, say what you think the article is about, why it exists and
  which passage told you.
- At the first unsupported step, pause and record the passage and the question it leaves.
  Continue reading and note whether the article later answers it, or whether you supplied
  the answer from your own knowledge.
- Notice effort even when you understand: values you had to remember, terms you had to
  reinterpret, a transition you reconstructed or a repeated lesson.
- Note passages that feel mannered or mechanical, and passages that work well. Give the
  actual wording and its effect, rather than a general impression.

At the end, say what you can now explain or decide, and what remains unclear. Keep this
account separate from suggestions about how to fix the page.

Return your original observations before receiving the voice guide for the rest of the
reader lens. Preserve useful passages as well as problems. A separate fresh visual
reader assesses the figures before seeing the prose.
