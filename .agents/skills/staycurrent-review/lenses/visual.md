# Visual lens

Tests both each figure's explanation and the learning supplied by their sequence.
Complete and return the [first visual reading](first-visual-reading.md) before reading
this file or the additional inputs below. The visual reader must be separate from the
prose-first reader: familiarity with the prose can hide missing visual explanations.

**Informed inputs:** the same fixed figures and full article; its brief, exit questions
and visual coverage map; the [figure guide](../../staycurrent-write/references/figures.md)
and [house style](../../../../docs/figure-style.md). An older article may lack a visual
map; judge its coverage directly against the brief and questions. Do not treat the
author's map as proof of completeness. Preserve the unaided account beside this review.

## Understanding and coverage

- Compare the unaided account with the intended explanation. Identify correct
  understanding, mistaken inferences and causal steps supplied by prior knowledge.
  For each figure, decide whether its setup, cause and outcome were understandable
  without the article or another figure. A correct guess is not evidence that the
  visual taught the lesson.
- Compare what the whole sequence taught with the article's main mechanisms,
  consequential failures and practical choices. Name the visual evidence for each
  major lesson and the gaps. Detailed code, derivations and references may remain in
  prose; missing central reasoning is a coverage finding, not an automatic exemption.
- Judge contribution and effort. Motion, variation, numbers and visual relationships
  should explain something useful. Flag a caption doing the drawing's work, needless
  activity, or controls that add effort without teaching. Recommend a simpler form,
  a combined example or another figure when that preserves or adds understanding.
- Read the figures in the article afterwards. Check consistency of terms, facts and
  results, and whether long text alternatives faithfully describe the visible lesson.
  Technical claims still receive the practitioner lens; do not invent guarantees from
  a plausible animation.

## Rendering and operation

Use the figure guide's rendered checks: initial-to-final playback, every control and
variant including keyboard use, house-style defects, both themes at desktop and 375 px,
and contrast. Record actual observations and unavailable checks; a screenshot proves
only its frame. Reuse valid checks for unchanged rendering and controls, but prior
prose-informed approval does not establish independent visual comprehension.

For a collection, check consistent role icons, identities, values and colour meanings
across the reading routes. Reuse article checks rather than reviewing every figure again.

## Result

Return a short evidence-based judgement of individual comprehension and sequence
coverage, with ranked findings and useful passages or visual choices to preserve.
Neither operation nor attractive rendering establishes that the explanation works.
False visual understanding is Critical; a missing causal step or major lesson is
Medium. Optional polish is Low. Report all findings, not only likely blockers.

If teaching is repaired, the review skill requires an unprimed reader for the affected
explanation; retain unaffected checks. A review-only commission stops with its findings.
