# Authoring and review workflow trial

4 October 2026. These are initial trials of the skills, not evidence that unattended
article production is solved. The [coverage plan](content-structure.md) remains the
scope for future commissions; no article batch was produced or published here.

Three independent design reviews informed the authoring and review skills. A fresh
author then used the authoring skill for a bounded prose-only explanation, and three
reviewers tested the review process on a deliberately defective rendered page.

## Authoring trial

The commission asked for a short explanation of why an additional index can save
lookup work while adding insertion work. The author produced a concrete orders
example, a claim-to-source record, and a proposed illustration. The illustration was
not implemented: this trial explicitly commissioned prose only.

Separate technical, editorial, and blind-comprehension reviews found the central
explanation successful. The blind reader correctly predicted that retrieving almost
every order can reduce the index's advantage, while recognising that an actual choice
needs evidence. The reviews also identified two useful refinements: define “index
page,” and state explicitly that the final example's extra index is unused. Those
remain recorded in the preserved trial, which was not edited after review. Tree
navigation could be more concrete in a fuller explanation.

## Review trial with planted defects

The isolated fixture is deliberately incorrect and must not be used as an article
or teaching reference. Three agents first interpreted its figure without the article
or intended answer. They then received their separate role inputs; the blind reader
received the page and audience only.

The reviews caught an impossible page count, an unsupported claim that indexes make
every query faster without insertion cost, controls that changed their selected label
but not their result, and prose that relied on the control to prove its conclusion.
Browser inspection confirmed that selecting columns still displayed four pages.

The trial also exposed an overly strict review judgement: an editor treated the lack
of an explicit all-fields answer as missing coverage, although the blind reader could
derive that answer. The protocol now asks reviewers to assess demonstrated transfer
of understanding rather than require every outcome to become another printed section.
The technical reviewer reconsidered the same concern after calibration; the original
editorial report is retained as evidence of the workflow's weakness.

## Subsequent user guidance

The skills now require first-impression review of code and interactive examples as
well as illustrations, with three independent readings per aid. Failed interpretations
lead to informed repair advice and fresh readers for meaningful revisions. The earlier
fixed two-repair limit was replaced by reassessing the approach after repeated failure;
a review count never establishes acceptance. These expanded paths have been inspected
but have not yet been exercised through a complete illustrated article and repair cycle.

The revision also covers media gaps across the whole article, headings and supporting
copy, prerequisite vocabulary, accessibility semantics, annotated further reading,
and evidence that needs revisiting. Writing remains guided by actual reference
passages rather than numerical scores or prohibited-word lists.

## What the checks establish

The official skill validator passes for all four project skills in both agent
directories, and the mirrored files match. The site builds all 18 pages. These checks
establish valid skill packaging and an intact site build, not editorial quality.

The trials supply useful evidence of narrow authoring and defect detection. They do
not establish full article flow, finished house artwork in both themes, accessibility
with assistive technology, repeatable repair success, or performance across many
subjects. A bounded article with actual supporting media is the next useful production
trial. Keep its results and improve the skills from observed failures.

## Guidance revision after the relational introduction

4 October 2026, after the whole-page polish. The earlier workflow descriptions above
are historical trial records. The user asked us to carry learning forward through
context, examples and desired outcomes, giving capable agents room to decide how
to achieve them.

Revised the planning, authoring, style, research and review skills and their relevant
references. The skills now explain the reader's desired understanding and the
reasoning behind useful decisions. Fixed reviewer teams, numerical repair triggers,
compulsory evidence-table formats and universal aid-review counts have given way to
review effort chosen for the actual uncertainty and any explicit user request.
Independent challenge, an uncued first reading, technical evidence and honest
verification remain part of the desired outcome. Publication boundaries are unchanged.

The worked examples preserve the important learning: orientation before a detailed
walkthrough when introducing an unfamiliar technology; depth supplied by a missing
causal step; transitions that continue the reasoning; an index question repaired so
its table lookup becomes necessary; and a static timeline that makes ordering
inspectable without a new interactive model. The writing reference includes actual
passages and their context rather than turning those passages into a prose formula.

A fresh agent used the revised skills for a bounded
[caching planning trial](workflow-validation/2026-10-04/skill-guidance/caching-trial.md).
It established caching before its thumbnail example, proposed a subject-specific
collection, chose a static hit/miss comparison and separated later production from
the current commission. It found repeated reference reading burdensome for a small
assignment. The skills now explicitly support choosing the relevant example or
reference instead of rereading the catalogue. This was a planning and sample-writing
trial, not production or validation of a finished caching article.

An [independent audit](workflow-validation/2026-10-04/skill-guidance/audit.md) found two
conflicts and rechecked their repairs. Linked editorial guidance still prescribed a
contrasting workload pair and a closing exercise; those are now contextual options.
Blind review also needed fresh-context launch guidance, and the style entrypoint
itself still contained case history. Coordinators now have the concrete fork setting,
and the entrypoint routes blind readers without exposing that history.

All five skills validate in both agent locations. Their mirrors and local reference
links match. These checks establish packaging and coherence, while the trial offers
limited evidence of the judgement the new guidance encourages. Continue learning
from actual articles rather than treating this revision as a finished authoring system.
