# Relational learning-tools repair

19 October 2026. **The collection is ready as drafts after repair.** All 54 pages
were assessed for what the reader needs to see or change. The three author
handoffs record each page's decision, including useful static examples and later
failure or operating cases. No article was added to reach this result.

The repair changes 51 draft bodies and retains suitable existing media in RL86,
RL95 and RL115. There are 64 new aid placements, including a static sparse-page
comparison and reused models. Placement counts describe the implementation; they
are not an acceptance criterion.

## What the reader can explore

- Join inputs, matching pairs, repeated totals and outer-join predicates.
- Retained transaction reads, pending writes, committed rows, lock ownership and
  continuous waiting, with different decisions and schedules.
- Recovery targets, missing accepted work, replay identity and coupled capacity.
- Replication receipt, flush, replay and the snapshot used for a read.
- Versions, cleanup, index methods, schema compatibility and authority boundaries.
- Key ranges and request paths, vote placement, retained history and diverging
  migration or upgrade histories.

The models keep relevant records and relationships visible. SQL and native
worksheets remain beside them as implementation evidence; browser models keep
their simplifications explicit. There is no requirement for every passage to
become interactive.

## How the judgement was reached

The [skill changes](skills.md) moved media decisions and challenges to missing
media into the main workflows. The original learning-tools finding remains in
`../review/learning-tools-gap.md`.

Independent readers first inspected rendered aids without the article or author
notes, saved predictions, and chose changes. The coordinator operated those exact
native controls because child sessions could not access the in-app browser. The
readers then compared outcomes and pixels, inspected the models and followed
actual article passages. Their reports preserve mistaken predictions as well as
implementation defects. They repaired the defects and rechecked the revised
artifacts. A further fresh reader tested the rebuilt introduction aid and its
article context.

The process found faults even where model tests passed: premature commit was
hidden by an artificial guard; captured IDs were recut by mutable sort keys;
window contributions were clipped; fixture changes and a resumed transaction
were unstated; dropped-column comparisons were impossible; quota and recovery
progress displays contradicted their authority conditions. The final phone
sweep also exposed hidden NULL outcomes and import dispositions. These were
repaired, rather than accepted because controls existed.
