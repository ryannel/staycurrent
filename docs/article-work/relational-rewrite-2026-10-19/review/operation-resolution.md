## Consequential findings and repairs

1. **RL91's count protocol was supported by the wrong reported observation.** The page shows a control-row lock followed by COUNT over booking rows, without a maintained counter. Its next paragraph said the native run returned `reserved = 2, capacity = 2`. The script and JSON do contain that counter read after a parent-row wait, but neither inserts the second booking in that particular trial. A reader cannot treat that as an executed counter-free count protocol. The article now explains the count result conditionally: when A inserts the second booking before releasing the control row, B's subsequent Read Committed count sees two. This follows the documented statement-snapshot rule and shared writer protocol. The false native attribution is removed. Recheck: distinguish the observed counter-after-wait trial from the documented reasoning for the displayed count variant; the text now does so. The variant was not independently executed in this review.

2. **RL91 named the wrong native deadlock victim.** Its original text claimed B received `40P01` and A completed. `rl91-native-observed.json` records A's `40P01` and B's successful pending UPDATE. The text now matches that recorded run and retains the warning that another schedule may choose another victim. Recheck: A holds s7, B holds s8, A requests s8, B requests s7; A's rejected attempt cannot retain earlier changes, and B's statement proceeds. No claim of a deterministic victim is made.

3. **RL100 gave a misleading reason for a short result from its exact batch query.** It suggested selected candidates become ineligible without explaining that the CTE locks them as it selects them and holds those locks through UPDATE. The repair states that lock lifetime and uses the applicable reason: eligible rows held by other workers can remain outside an empty or short batch. PostgreSQL SELECT's lock/limit rules and the retained locked-ID5 trial support the distinction. Recheck: a held eligible row is skipped, an empty result does not prove global completion, and a later batch after release repairs it. The final maintained writer rule and validated equality remain the completion proof.

4. **RL103's result lacked its diagnostic assignment.** `reserved = 1` after rollback was interpretable as another actual booking admission, although the script deliberately assigns counter values to expose a lock. The native section now says A assigns 0 and B's UPDATE assigns/returns 1, and directs the booking handler to its guarded reservation protocol. Recheck: B's returned 1 is a diagnostic assignment, not a freshly derived capacity guarantee. The observed cancellation/wait/rollback sequence is unchanged.

5. **RL103 Figure 2 advertised stepping without controls.** Root's rendered check found the shell already displayed all three states. It is now deliberately static: its `data-step-figure` marker is removed. Keeping cancel accepted and lock still held alongside later rollback makes their difference easier to compare. The numbered child blocks remain readable static content. No new interaction is implied; root owns final rendered confirmation.

6. **RL105 reused b17 as a new booking without resetting its meaning.** Earlier pages establish b17 as the already-existing booking. The routing model and retained evidence both use b17 among the three routed writes. The page now announces a separate routing fixture in which b17 does not yet exist, before describing the purchase. This preserves exact agreement with the evidence and makes the changed starting state visible. Recheck: the reader can distinguish the RL90 capacity history from this ownership-counting illustration; no silent duplication of an existing booking is required.

7. **RL109 appeared to rewrite the agreed charge it had been teaching readers to preserve.** Its movement example revised o81 from 4,000 to 4,500 without identifying a different mutable fact. The movement fixture now follows an editable fulfilment estimate carried alongside the retained agreed total. The table caption identifies estimates, the paragraph preserves o81's agreed 4,000 charge, and the model section maps its generic `amount` field to the estimate. The update/delete/insert, equal-count trap and exact values remain unchanged. Recheck: catalogue/history readers still retain 4,000 as the agreed charge while the copied mutable estimate advances to revision 2. The model only records the followed estimate; it does not test a business-history implementation.
