## Findings

No Critical finding established.

- **Medium — `pg-architecture`, Figure 5, “A matching key still needs a visible tuple”; `pg-plans`, Figure 1, “A three-row lookup, repeated thousands of times”.** Both steppers show almost all the information before playback: the lookup route in the first, all the row/loop counts in the second. Next largely moves the emphasis and replaces the explanatory paragraph. In the plan figure, no rows enter or accumulate in the repeated inner operation; the reader must infer the multiplication from the already printed numbers. Four clicks expose four paragraphs rather than a visible mechanism. Keep the useful route/nesting as a static annotated drawing, or make one row/request visibly traverse the route and accumulate the repeated work. The static nested-buffer and estimate-bar figures later in the plan article already achieve more with less interaction.

- **Medium — `pg-json`, Figure 2, “One field through extraction and validation”; `pg-access`, Figure 2, “Existing rows and proposed rows”.** These are useful input/answer comparisons wrapped in pseudo-process playback. JSON extraction initially withholds both values, then exposes them, then the contract result. The RLS figure spends a whole state on the ordinary privilege, then an existing-row result, then the proposed-row check; in the default SELECT the latter has no proposed row. Both explicitly disclaim a physical execution schedule, yet arrows and Play invite that reading. Selecting the input or statement is the useful interaction. Show the corresponding extraction/check/result together, retaining the counterexamples; remove the separate playback or justify it with a causal change that the reader can see. This also keeps controls and the answer closer together in the tall RLS figure.

- **Medium — `pg-json`, Figure 4, “The operator must reach the indexed expression”.** Three vertically stacked cards each repeat request → operator → index, taking roughly a screen and a half at the reviewed desktop size. The reader must scroll to compare whole-document GIN, expression GIN and numeric B-tree, although the distinction is a three-row mapping. A compact query-expression / operator / matching-index table preserves every teaching relationship and permits direct comparison. Keep the predicates and expressions intact; their precise differences are the lesson.

- **Medium — `pg-upgrades`, Figure 2, “Which files can the old server use?”.** Copy and Link change text in the same old-cluster / action / new-cluster boxes. There are no drawn file objects or references: “two names lead to different files” and “same underlying user files” must be reconstructed from prose. The displayed P7 values are equal in both modes and cannot explain the safety difference. Show the two directories pointing to one shared file versus two independent files, then expose what startup changes. This is a case where a small physical drawing would replace several repeated labels while teaching the reason for the boundary.

- **Medium — `replicas`, Figure 3, “A later read on an earlier copy”.** The two replica prices stay fixed, while the result of routing is communicated through changing “Read 1 on A”, “Read 2 on B” and session text. The central cause—one reader moving between copies—is not drawn. A visible reader/request route from A to B, or a compact two-read sequence with the retained-history alternative beside it, would make the backwards observation apparent without requiring the caption to supply the connection. Preserve the same histories and fresh snapshots: they make this distinct from changing data or snapshot retention.

- **Low — `pg-schema-change`, Figures 2–3; `pg-distribution`, Figure 6; `pg-upgrades`, Figure 3.** The current state, a short conclusion strip, a stage heading and the explanatory paragraph often restate the same transition. For example the final index-build state prints “Build completed”, “Valid”, `indisvalid = true`, “Validate the index → test representative query results and work”, and a further completion paragraph. Keep the necessary native flags and acceptance qualification, but consolidate the repeated explanation layers. These sequences have useful changes and need not be removed wholesale.

- **Low — `backups`, Figure 2, “The stopping point changes every later fact”.** At the reviewed desktop rendering, the provider box ends immediately against the current-state explanation, with nested borders competing for emphasis. Give the outside-provider history and the selected-cut consequence a clearer separation. The distinction and values themselves remain legible.
