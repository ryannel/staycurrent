Regional placement decides where a shard’s copies live and where its requests run. Put its voting replicas close together and they can agree quickly. Spread them across regions and a regional outage can leave enough voters to continue, but agreement may have to cross the network between regions. The number of copies alone does not tell you either result. Three voters in one region can survive a machine failure and all disappear in one regional failure. A customer’s order can sit near the application while its purchase still waits for stock owned elsewhere. This article gives the shop from Sharding and distributed SQL a separate regional layout. North, West and East are invented regions. Mina’s customer shard A has its leader in North; the shared product shard P has its leader in West. We first place the voters for one shard, then follow the work that purchase 103 needs. The drawings and network times are illustrative models, not observations from a running cluster. Regions and failure domains Counting the surviving voters What a regional write waits for A local order can need remote stock Global constraints have a location Reading near the application Choosing and checking a placement Regions and failure domains A failure domain is a set of resources that one incident can remove together. A failed machine removes the replicas on that machine. A zone outage can remove several machines sharing power or connectivity. A region outage can remove several zones. Placing copies on different machines helps only against failures that leave those machines independent. These names describe the failures you intend to tolerate. Check the actual dependencies behind them: separate zone labels do not make a shared network or power supply independent. The same applies to the application. Surviving database voters are little use to a request if its only application server has disappeared. Keep three locations distinct. The request origin is where the application sends the request. The owner’s leader handles the authoritative work for a shard in this model. Its voting replicas participate in agreement on that shard’s history. Moving the request origin does not move either the leader or its voters. Counting the surviving voters The parent article established a fixed group of three Raft voters whose majority is two. Here we ask which failures leave that majority. Start with three voters in North in Figure 1. Remove one North zone, then the whole of North. Retaining history and accepting new writes are different promises. A group without a quorum may retain every earlier committed change and still stop new writes. A non-voting replica can add a copy, and may support qualified reads, but it does not add a vote to this calculation.
