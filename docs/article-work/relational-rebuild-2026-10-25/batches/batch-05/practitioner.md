## Coverage and judgement

**Retries and uncertain commit outcomes.** The complete protocol distinguishes a known aborted attempt, a committed business refusal, a persistent error and an unknown commit. The request identity is caller-retained, customer/operation-scoped and distinct from the order ID. Canonical payload comparison prevents changed intent from borrowing an earlier result. The unique insert is the purchase gate; the duplicate branch reads in a separate Read Committed statement, with the invisible-conflict caveat explained. The savepoint sits after claiming, so a mug refusal can undo the prior tea decrement while retaining a terminal refusal. An unexpected SQL error instead rolls the entire attempt back. A null result is explicitly forbidden at commit, rather than presented as a recoverable committed job state.

Recovery re-enters this gate, including when an original attempt may still be running. It does not infer failure from absence, replica lag, a stale snapshot, cancellation dispatch or an exhausted caller budget. The retained creation answer remains distinct from the order's later state. Whole-transaction retries include decision logic and retain the same key. Retention, expired-key policy and the external-effect boundary are substantive limits, not an unqualified exactly-once promise.

**Outboxes and database-backed jobs.** The order and fixed acknowledgement intention share one transaction. Sending before or after a direct database commit has a concrete counterexample. The work ID, purchase token and worker generation remain distinct. Receiver acceptance and local completion are separate facts; both sides of the duplicate window are explained, including ordinary email without a deduplication contract. The receiver owns atomic deduplication, parameter comparison and retained outcome evidence. Redrive after that evidence expires is correctly treated as a reconciliation problem.

The claim CTE locks eligible rows before changing owner/generation/deadline, limits the batch and commits before external work. `SKIP LOCKED` is explained as an incomplete queue observation without ordering/fairness guarantees. Completion locks first, then samples wall-clock expiry in its guarded update; owner, generation and unexpired lease are required. Renewal follows the same protocol. Expiry permits takeover without stopping old processes or in-flight calls. Fencing is explicitly receiver-enforced, scoped to one protected resource, atomic with the effect, and limited both for equal generations and before the receiver learns the newer generation. Per-job counters are not incorrectly promoted to a shared-resource order. Business sequencing, event versus command validity, cancellation, deadlines, failed work, deliberate redrive and queue observability are covered at useful practitioner depth.
