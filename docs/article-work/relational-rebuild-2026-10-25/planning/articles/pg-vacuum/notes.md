## Primary sources checked, 25 October 2026

Use the PostgreSQL 18 documentation as the versioned baseline; inspect effective settings on any observed server.

- [Routine Vacuuming](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/routine-vacuuming.html): cleanup horizons, freezing, anti-wraparound intervention, MultiXact differences and automatic eligibility. In an urgent XID incident the documented remedy is ordinary vacuum after resolving retention, not FULL or indiscriminate FREEZE. The routine-vacuuming prose has apparent column-name slips (`transactionid`, `relminxid`); use the catalogue definitions below for working SQL. The prepared view column is `transaction`; table XID age uses `relfrozenxid`.
- [VACUUM](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-vacuum.html): optional ANALYZE, index-cleanup choices, page skipping, tail truncation and FULL's new copy, spare space and ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock. Plain vacuum normally admits ordinary reads and writes; truncation can need the stronger lock. Do not describe it as having no lock or resource impact.
- [Visibility Map](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/storage-vm.html) and [Free Space Map](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/storage-fsm.html): reusable capacity and visibility/freezing proofs answer different questions. All-visible and all-frozen are separate bits; vacuum sets proofs when safe and modifying work can clear them.
- [Cumulative Statistics](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/monitoring-stats.html): `n_dead_tup` is an estimate, counters lag and can be cached within the monitoring transaction; `backend_xid`, `backend_xmin` and feedback horizons supply different evidence. Refresh observations appropriately. Estimated dead tuples are not a measured bloat percentage.
- [VACUUM Progress](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/progress-reporting.html#VACUUM-PROGRESS-REPORTING): heap scan, index cycles and final phases do distinct work; a scanned-heap percentage is not overall completion. FULL uses `pg_stat_progress_cluster`.
- [pg_replication_slots](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/view-pg-replication-slots.html), [Hot Standby](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/hot-standby.html#HOT-STANDBY-CONFLICT): distinguish tuple/catalogue horizons from WAL `restart_lsn`; standby feedback can retain primary tuples. Identify the cause here and explain why removing retention has a consumer cost; retention policy and replica rebuild procedures stay with their owners.
- [PREPARE TRANSACTION](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-prepare-transaction.html), [pg_prepared_xacts](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/view-pg-prepared-xacts.html): prepared work can retain storage and locks after leaving its session. Resolution must follow the transaction coordinator's outcome rather than assuming that disconnect cancels it.
- [pg_class](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/catalog-pg-class.html), [pg_database](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/catalog-pg-database.html): correct XID/MultiXact age columns; database values summarise the oldest table obligations. A manual command that skips catalogues or other oldest relations cannot establish database repair.
- [Vacuum configuration](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-vacuum.html): effective triggers, worker availability, cost delay and freezing settings. PostgreSQL 18's update/delete threshold is capped by `autovacuum_vacuum_max_threshold` when enabled; use the formula in Routine Vacuuming rather than silently assuming an uncapped percentage rule. Insert eligibility and age triggers are separate. The shared cost-delay budget can be balanced across workers, so extra workers do not imply proportional extra throughput. Failsafe may bypass index cleanup to advance age. No universal tuning values are prescribed.
- [pgstattuple](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/pgstattuple.html): optional deeper physical evidence separates dead tuple bytes and free space. A full scan has cost and its results are affected by concurrent changes. Extension installation and general privilege management are not this article's teaching target.
