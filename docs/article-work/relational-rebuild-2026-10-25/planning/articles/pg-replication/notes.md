## Primary sources checked

PostgreSQL 18 documentation, checked 26 October 2026:

- [Log-shipping standby servers](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby.html): compatible physical copies, standby startup and WAL sources, streaming, physical slots and the promotion input boundary. Cascading standbys are only orientation here; their transport remains asynchronous.
- [pg_basebackup](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/app-pgbasebackup.html): full-cluster starting copy, replication access and sender capacity; `-R` writes standby configuration. Backup-chain validation and PITR stay with `pg-backup`.
- [Monitoring statistics](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/monitoring-stats.html#MONITORING-PG-STAT-REPLICATION-VIEW): sender `sent_lsn`/`write_lsn`/`flush_lsn`/`replay_lsn` and `sync_state`; receiver `written_lsn` is not a data-integrity check, while `flushed_lsn` includes sync. Recent lag intervals include feedback delay; idle nulls are not a fault or a catch-up forecast. The native views do not supply a simple volatile-receipt-only position matching the parent's conceptual first stage.
- [Administration functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/functions-admin.html#FUNCTIONS-RECOVERY-CONTROL): `pg_last_wal_receive_lsn()` means received and synced; distinguish replay and role checks. Pause requests need actual-state confirmation; streaming during a pause can consume space. `pg_promote(wait => true)` reports completion or timeout; sending a promotion signal is weaker evidence. [WAL position functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/functions-admin.html#FUNCTIONS-ADMIN-BACKUP) distinguish insert, write and flush and provide byte differences.
- [WAL settings](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-wal.html#GUC-SYNCHRONOUS-COMMIT): all non-off levels retain local flush; configured standbys distinguish remote write, durable flush and apply. Without synchronous names, remote levels collapse to local flush. Storage must honour its flush contract.
- [Replication settings](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-replication.html): FIRST/ANY and application names; retention limits, receiver feedback and standby delay controls. Avoid saying an ANY acknowledgement quorum elects a writer or that any listed copy is safe to promote. Settings that weaken the remote wait change the service promise.
- [Physical slot state](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/view-pg-replication-slots.html): `restart_lsn`, `wal_status`, `safe_wal_size` and active state. `confirmed_flush_lsn` is logical-only; it is NULL for physical slots. A storage cap can remove the ability to resume.
- [Hot standby](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/hot-standby.html): commit replay admits changes to new snapshots; recovery conflicts can delay replay or cancel reads. Delay budgets are shared replay budgets, not query runtimes. Feedback reduces cleanup conflicts by retaining tuples upstream; other conflict causes remain. This owns the policy delegated from `pg-vacuum`; detailed vacuum internals stay there.
- [Failover](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/warm-standby-failover.html): PostgreSQL supplies promotion, while external machinery detects failure and excludes the old writer. [pg_rewind](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/app-pgrewind.html): same-cluster divergence, target WAL and shutdown requirements, checksums or prior `wal_log_hints`, `full_page_writes`, subsequent recovery and configuration checks. Preserve evidence first; failed rewind can require a fresh copy.
