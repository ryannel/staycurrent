## Primary sources checked on 25 October 2026

- [Lamport, Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System (1978)](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/time-clocks.pdf), pp. 558–561: local/message order and logical-clock advancement. Causal order implies increasing clock values; the converse does not follow. No vector-clock tutorial or formal clock proof is commissioned.
- [Kulkarni et al., Logical Physical Clocks and Consistent Snapshots in Globally Distributed Databases (2014)](https://www.cse.buffalo.edu/tech-reports/2014-04.pdf), sections 1–3: HLC combines physical proximity with one-way causal ordering. This explains the components; it does not establish every database guarantee from the clock alone.
- [Corbett et al., Spanner: Google's Globally Distributed Database (2013)](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/pdf/65b514eda12d025585183a641b5a9e096a3c4be5.pdf), sections 3, 4.1.2–4.1.4: bounded time, commit waiting and safe-time eligibility. Prepared transactions can limit safe time independently of applied replication progress. This is a named protocol example, not a universal timestamp algorithm. The publisher-hosted 2013 version was accessible; the older archive URL failed.
- [Spanner: TrueTime and external consistency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/true-time-external-consistency): distinguishes serialization from real-time ordering and explains why an earlier completed transaction matters to a later read. Do not generalise product-specific uses of “strong consistency”.
- [CockroachDB Transaction Layer](https://docs.cockroachlabs.com/docs/stable/architecture/transaction-layer), “Time and hybrid logical clocks”, “Read snapshots”, “Closed timestamps”, “Transaction conflicts”: HLC exchange; uncertainty can require timestamp advancement and checks; a closure promise must be coupled with application of its specified replication position. Use only the conceptual contrast here; native restart details belong to the CockroachDB owners.
- [CockroachDB Follower Reads](https://docs.cockroachlabs.com/docs/stable/follower-reads): exact and bounded staleness are distinct contracts; historical reads remain consistent. “Follower” alone does not imply a stale read, because strong follower-read arrangements also exist. Avoid a universal claim that only leaders can serve current reads.
- [CockroachDB AS OF SYSTEM TIME](https://docs.cockroachlabs.com/docs/stable/as-of-system-time): retained history limits historical queries; engine syntax and native limits belong to `crdb-reads`. No fixed retention duration, follower-read delay or clock-offset default is needed here.
