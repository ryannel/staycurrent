## Primary-source checks, 25 October 2026

- [MariaDB application-time periods](https://mariadb.com/docs/server/reference/sql-structure/temporal-tables/application-time-periods): application periods and period splitting establish that changing part of a period can require retained unaffected portions. [Bitemporal tables](https://mariadb.com/docs/server/reference/sql-structure/temporal-tables/bitemporal-tables): combines application and system periods. These are concrete product examples, not portable syntax promises.
- [SQL Server temporal tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/tables/temporal/overview?view=sql-server-ver17): current/history tables retain previous row values; system periods use UTC transaction begin times. This supports the caution against equating every recorded timestamp with visibility or business effective time. [MariaDB system-versioned tables](https://mariadb.com/docs/server/reference/sql-structure/temporal-tables/system-versioned-tables) separately documents transaction-precise history, reinforcing that these semantics must be checked.
- [PostgreSQL 18 range types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/rangetypes.html): inclusive/exclusive bounds, unbounded ranges and exclusion-based non-overlap. The article adopts half-open periods explicitly; it must not imply non-overlap alone guarantees coverage or that a scalar UNIQUE constraint prevents overlap. In a bitemporal model, require the appropriate effective non-overlap within each recorded-time view, not across all superseded assertions.
- [PostgreSQL 18 constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/ddl-constraints.html): foreign-key delete actions and relationship semantics. A cascade reaches constrained referencing rows, not unrelated external files. The supplied mandatory customer reference means a physical profile-row delete requires an explicit schema/policy choice; do not casually replace it with NULL or alter the shared schema.
- [PostgreSQL 18 routine vacuuming](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/routine-vacuuming.html): updated/deleted physical row versions can remain until no transaction needs them. Neither SQL DELETE nor VACUUM should be presented as certified physical erasure, and MVCC retention is not the application's historical record.
- [ICO storage limitation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/) and [right to erasure](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-erasure/): retention depends on purpose; erasure can involve recipients and backups, and exceptions exist. Use these only to ground why engineering needs an agreed policy and copy inventory. Both pages currently say the guidance is under review. Do not prescribe a universal period or determine a shop-specific legal outcome. Backup handling can differ from live deletion and needs a defined expiry/beyond-use arrangement.
