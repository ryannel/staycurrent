## Findings

1. **Medium — give replica agreement a usable generic owner before it becomes a prerequisite.** Location: collection map, `distribution`, `replicas`, `distributed-commit`, `placement` and `cluster-change`; direct route “Decide whether and how to distribute data”. `distributed-commit`, `placement` and `cluster-change` require replica agreement, but `replicas` owns copied-history progress/read routing and `distribution` owns partition/shard distinctions, locality and coordination. The only explicit teaching owner for agreement is `crdb-architecture`. Consequently a newcomer following the generic distribution route cannot yet explain why an isolated minority cannot acknowledge authoritative writes, why merely counting copies does not establish survival, or how replica agreement differs from an atomic transaction across independently replicated partitions. Requiring the CockroachDB article would insert an unannounced implementation detour into transferable judgement. Give `distribution` the compact foundation in authoritative replicated history, voting agreement and the availability consequence of losing a quorum; keep failure-domain arithmetic with `placement`, membership procedures with `cluster-change`, and native Raft/range details with `crdb-architecture`. Alternatively name another generic owner and route to it before these prerequisites. This needs a bounded parent-level explanation, not a new consensus article or the wider distributed-systems programme. The distinction matters in the documented CockroachDB replication contract: quorum agreement protects each range’s replicated changes; it is not supplied by having multiple copies alone. [CockroachDB replication layer](https://docs.cockroachlabs.com/docs/stable/architecture/replication-layer).

2. **Low — point the slow-request route to both native CockroachDB evidence owners.** Location: direct route “Fix a slow request”, its native-evidence note, and `crdb-operations`/`crdb-performance`. The note names only `crdb-operations`, whose brief concentrates on secure identities, under-replication, range health and node removal. SQL/KV work, fan-out and contention evidence belong to `crdb-performance`. A reader with an expensive request should see that native branch directly, rather than opening the cluster-health page first. Clarify the route; no merge or ownership transfer is needed.
