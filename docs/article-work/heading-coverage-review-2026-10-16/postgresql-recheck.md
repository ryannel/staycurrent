## Findings

No material heading-scope, transition or anchor defect remains in the changes checked. The replacements name mechanisms that the existing body actually explains. They do not require new technical coverage merely because they use a familiar technical term. The remaining question-style headings were retained where they already gave a useful expectation.

- **architecture:** “Vacuum and maintenance after a commit” contains retained versions, visibility, freezing, statistics and maintenance eligibility. The opening ties the work to the just-completed stock update. “Maintenance” correctly contains the additional ANALYZE discussion; the title does not imply that vacuum runs synchronously at commit.
- **extensions:** the four replacements accurately distinguish exclusion constraints, temporal foreign-key coverage, PostGIS geography and installation/maintenance boundaries. The transitions already explain the change from overlap to complete coverage, then from booking rules to geographic discovery. The spatial section does not claim that finding a suitable room reserves its time. “Installing and maintaining extensions” contains package availability, database objects, privileges, dependencies and upgrades, rather than promising a package-manager tutorial.
- **full-text-search:** “Storing weighted search vectors” names the generated title/body representation that the section develops after establishing the document fixture. “GIN candidate matching” matches the lexeme retrieval, heap access and recheck discussion; the body preserves the distinction from ranked results. No new hierarchy is needed for these short, sequential investigations.
- **indexes:** the B-tree, operator-class, GIN, BRIN and maintenance labels each anticipate the actual mechanism. The GIN section explains both posting lists and their posting-tree extension, so its familiar label is a useful entry point rather than an exclusion of the latter. BRIN's page-range label contains the ordered/scattered comparison and the maintenance of summaries. The new tree is substantially more useful for returning to an access method than the original sequence of imperatives.
- **json:** “Matching conditions within one array element” expresses why independent seller and price matches fail. “GIN indexes on JSON expressions” fits the whole-value versus extracted-tags comparison. “Validating and indexing numeric attributes” contains both the tolerant guarded read and the stricter write policy, so it does not silently equate query filtering with input validation.
- **query-execution:** parsing/planning, custom/generic plans, streaming/blocking execution, working memory/parallelism and total request time are all explained in their sections. The custom-plan section begins with its prepared SQL before the definition; the preceding section has already established the retained session and uneven tenant fixture, so this existing transition remains intelligible. The streaming label contains the five-row comparison and the broader startup examples. No subsection has been relabelled as a topic that appears only in passing.
- **recovery:** the renamed sections follow WAL progress, a crash experiment, page-specific redo, checkpoints/full-page images, acknowledgement settings and point-in-time recovery. The last section's retention discussion supports recoverability rather than changing its subject. “Crash recovery with older data pages” is more accurate than treating a whole table file as one recovery age. The body still distinguishes page progress from transaction outcome.
- **replication:** the map now exposes physical versus logical replication, slots, synchronous acknowledgement, replica identity, fencing and logical continuity through failover. The read-freshness section actually includes configured synchronous-commit experiments and their limits. Its final standby-conflict discussion remains relevant to preserving a readable copy. “Logical replication and replica identity” is broad enough for independent schema/sequence responsibilities and apply conflicts; it does not reduce the section to only identity metadata.
- **row-versions:** snapshot visibility, long-lived readers, HOT, visibility-map scans, vacuum diagnosis and write skew have distinct bodies and useful new landmarks. The sequence from retained heap payload to index references remains explicit in the preceding paragraph. “Snapshot isolation and write skew” matches the two-doctor example and the Serializable comparison; it does not imply that snapshot selection alone preserves that invariant.
- **sharding:** the new foreign-table H2 gives a genuine separate mechanism its own place. The previous partition section now ends after local uniqueness. The new opening explicitly says the preceding example remains on one server, then explains remote tables, pushdown and independent transaction semantics. The next “Tenant keys and relationships” section establishes correct identity before Citus placement. The introductory preview now includes the local/remote distinction. Two-phase commit, rebalancing and compatibility labels all describe their existing bodies. The short FDW section is enough for the promised boundary; it does not purport to teach complete FDW administration.
- **vector-search:** the HNSW label names the graph mechanism developed after the exact-search comparison; a closing IVFFlat orientation does not make that scope misleading. “Tenant filters and iterative HNSW scans” identifies the actual experiment and its response to rejected candidates. “Measuring recall against an exact result” accurately includes identity comparison, strict ordering, variation between builds and the distinction from semantic relevance.
