# Relational animation redesign

Commission: replace the relational collection's animations with the approved
application-caching visual language. This is a correction to the narrower
visual-economy repair at `31a3c4b7`, not another prose or database-evidence review.
The existing instruction to repair and publish remains the delivery authority.

## Delivery scope

- 140 authored scene definitions render 141 animation instances across 57
  articles. PostgreSQL plans and JSON retain their static comparisons and
  direct-input tools; neither had an animation to replace.
- Every former stepped player is absent from the built relational articles.
  Exact tables, plans, maps and useful calculators remain where inspection is
  the lesson.
- The shared drawing vocabulary directly reuses caching's coloured balls,
  travel easing, subtle shadows, fine paths and familiar role icons. Each
  mechanism has its own geometry and causal sequence.
- Scenes play once as they enter view and settle. They retain useful comparison
  choices, support Pause/Replay, and provide a static outcome and text alternative
  without motion. Replay retains the chosen options. Only the current article's
  scene modules load in the browser.
- Prose changes remove obsolete stepping instructions and drawing references.
  Existing SQL, fixtures and native execution evidence are unchanged.

See [foundations](foundations.md), [PostgreSQL](postgresql.md), and
[distributed systems](distributed.md) for inventories and independent checks.

## Verification

The coordinator checked rendered text bounds and collisions at desktop and
375 px across the foundations/PostgreSQL scenes, all alternatives, and initial,
quarter, middle, three-quarter and final states. The distributed worker made
the equivalent sweep for its 36 scenes. Checks used both site themes and
screenshots of actual article and review pages. Long labels, overlapping status
notes and misplaced labels were repaired; affected states were checked again.
The development-only review route supplies reproducible still frames and is
excluded from production builds.

Independent source checks compared the new drawings with their preserved models
and article explanations. Repairs include outbox completion timing, minimal-cover
reduction, lossless reconstruction's default variant, explicit attempted loyalty
codes, missing-WAL replay, the PostgreSQL consistency-WAL boundary, and completing
recovery before accepting a reconciliation write. These are scoped confirmations
of changed visuals; native measurements were not rerun.

All 140 definitions and declared alternative combinations render at 21 time
points: 5,901 scene outputs with no exceptions or invalid numeric coordinates.
The integrated build checks all public routes, links, figure numbering and
theme-safe structure. The 24 site-check tests pass. The pre-existing 36 prose
warnings across 16 pages remain; no new structural errors remain.

Runtime checking uses the production preview to avoid development hot reloads
resetting animation state. A concrete control issue was found there: clicking
Replay below a tall figure left too little of the stage visible for the old
observer threshold. Relational scenes now require 35% visible area, still pause
offscreen and in hidden tabs, and retain the caching behaviour for its own scenes.

On the production preview, selecting the paid-only index with a required heap
visibility check and pressing Replay by keyboard retained `partial:check`,
advanced from 0 to 1, and settled at one entry and one table fetch with no console
errors. Keyboard Pause exposes Resume and holds the frame. The final release
build passed; the built collection contains 141 new players and zero legacy
step players. Reduced-motion handling reuses the existing caching player; its
final-state rendering was checked, without changing the host's motion setting.
