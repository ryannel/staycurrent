### teaching-1 · medium · r10 · “Put the query in the key”, first paragraph and Figure 2

Problem: The paragraph introduces later orders 105 and 106, belonging to Mina and Edda at the same instant, and says “Equal timestamps make the tie-breaker necessary.” The displayed query filters `customer_id = 1`. Edda's 106 is excluded, while Mina's qualifying 105 and 103 have different timestamps. Consequently the example contains no equal-time result rows whose order the ID settles.

Effect: The newcomer cannot derive the claimed need for the tie-breaker from the worked query. They must supply an unstated same-customer tie, or may mistakenly think equal timestamps in excluded customers make the filtered result ambiguous. This is a local explanation gap in the condition under which the ordering rule changes, rather than an objection to including a deterministic ID order.

Suggestion: State the general future-tie reason explicitly and add a small, clearly separate same-customer tied-order case if demonstrating it here. Preserve the canonical ownership of 105 and 106. Alternatively, narrow the claim about what these two rows demonstrate and leave a linked, prepared tie example to its existing owner.

Evidence: [Frozen r10 section](http://127.0.0.1:4398/review/relational-collection/indexes/#design-from-the-query). The paragraph assigns 105 to Mina and 106 to Edda; the SQL filters customer 1; Figure 2 and “Read this comparison as text” pass 106 because it belongs to customer 3, then return 105 and 103. The canonical example also fixes the two customers and common timestamp.
