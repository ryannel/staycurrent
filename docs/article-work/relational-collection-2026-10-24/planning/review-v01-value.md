## Findings

### value-1 · medium · Incident triage comes after mechanisms that many incidents do not need

Location: `v01/routes.md:27` (“Diagnose slow service”), with `v01/tree.yaml:999` (RL103, especially “The request's missing time” and “A chain of waits”), `v01/tree.yaml:1061` (RL102), and `v01/tree.yaml:905` (RL87).

Problem: the explicit general route begins RL86 → RL87, then proceeds through RL102 to RL103. RL92 is correctly conditional, but indexes, execution plans and capacity remain four named openings before the reader reaches the article that distinguishes pool delay, lock waits and expensive execution. The route's stated entry already includes basic locks and application pools. A slow request held behind one transaction can therefore be sent through access paths, query operators and capacity budgeting before discovering that none explains its delay. A reader with those concepts already can skip, but this does not help the intended newcomer who is being told which understanding their current task needs.

Effect: the collection spends reader effort on two or three unrelated mechanisms before establishing the causal branch. It also delays the useful decision between inspecting a blocker, controlling admission and repairing a plan. This is a task-route cost, not an objection to the four articles' existence or to their parentage.

Suggestion: make RL103's initial time-attribution/triage explanation the first entry for this task. It should provide enough local orientation to choose a branch: RL91 for unfamiliar lock reasoning, RL102 for admission/queue saturation, RL86/RL87 for expensive query work, RL92 for retention, followed by the relevant native evidence. Understanding lost: none required by the lock-only or queue-only task. Access-path, plan and capacity explanations remain intact for readers whose evidence selects them; the repair requires retaining enough triage orientation to avoid guessing at the branch. No article cut or merge is requested.
