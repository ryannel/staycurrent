# Heading wording calibration

Reviewed the 30 headings in the supplied packet against the publication’s stated reference naming and audience. No body text or other sources were consulted. Individual judgements, exact headings, scores and any suggested replacements are in `results.json`.

The results are **18 keep, 4 revise and 8 context needed**. These are wording judgements, not approval of unseen sections.

Direct technical names fit well when they identify a useful subject: “Functional dependencies”, “Two-phase commit” and “Normal forms” are strong examples. A short noun is not inherently too terse: “Keys” and “Pages” are viable headings when the article establishes their domain. Task and explanatory headings also fit: “Checking an index with EXPLAIN” specifies task, object and tool; “What COMMIT waits for” poses a concrete behavioural question. “How much to draw” and “Choosing what may be lost” show that ordinary conversational language can name a useful concern.

The revisions address actual wording: “retention responsibility” is strained and abstract; “A pool is a budget” asserts a metaphorical equivalence; “Paying for the extra route” combines metaphors without naming the object; and “The checks called normal forms” puts loose framing ahead of the recognisable term. Suggested replacements retain only information supplied by the heading. No replacement is proposed for “Paying for the extra route”, because the underlying subject is not specified.

Context-dependent judgements are deliberately unresolved. Pronouns and definite references such as “it” and “the numbers” can work with a nearby antecedent. “Jump” and “walk”, “same-page shortcut”, and “Fork and stair” may be precise in an established example or diagram, but their wording alone does not demonstrate that. Similarly, “Prepare is a promise” needs an established operation and meaning of “promise” before its precision can be assessed.

Scores describe confidence in the wording’s fit with the reference naming, not technical correctness or section quality. A low score with `context_needed` records weak standalone identification; it does not establish that the heading must change. Even strong subject labels may be too broad or too narrow for an unseen section. No conclusions about surrounding prose, technical claims, provenance or expected outcomes follow from this exercise.
