# Heading review calibration · 24 October 2026

One fresh reviewer received 30 shuffled, unlabelled headings: ten from the fixed reference articles, ten previously published headings selected from the recent correction, and their ten accepted revisions. The answer key was frozen before assignment and withheld. The reviewer had no conversation history, drafts, body text, previous verdicts or repository access by instruction. It reported consulting only the packet. The exact prompt, packet, provenance key and raw results are retained here.

| Provenance | Keep | Revise | Context needed | Mean fit score / 5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fixed references | 8 | 0 | 2 | 4.1 |
| Previous headings | 0 | 4 | 6 | 2.7 |
| Accepted revisions | 10 | 0 | 0 | 4.7 |

Every revision scored higher than its previous counterpart. The reviewer kept conversational reference headings such as “How much to draw” and “What COMMIT waits for”, alongside “Pages” and “B-tree”. It rejected “A pool is a budget”, “A slot is a retention responsibility”, “Paying for the extra route” and “The checks called normal forms”. It requested context for six previous headings, including “Follow what determines what” and “Prepare is a promise”. The two reference headings needing context were “Where the numbers come from” and “Fork and stair”.

The result demonstrates discrimination in this small wording test, rather than reliable full-article approval. The reviewer did not outright reject all ten previous headings. Uncertainty was reasonable without the section: the reference examples themselves show why a general ban on context, questions or figurative language would be wrong. Its proposed “Pools and budgets” also shows that identifying a weak heading does not establish the best repair.

The existing editorial check now asks reviewers to judge the actual wording against reference examples and resolve doubtful wording in the available section. Inferring the intended subject does not establish good writing. The review entrypoint now makes acceptance rest on concrete examples and reasoning against the references, while coverage records what was checked. These are changes to the existing review and coordinator acceptance, with no extra reviewer, pass matrix or review round.

The test used a focused assignment stating the naming criterion and asking for actual-wording judgements, rather than loading the entire review skill or revealing the full references and their target headings. This is not an end-to-end validation of the revised skill, a repeatability estimate, or evidence that the former skill alone caused the earlier misses. No article was rewritten or reviewed again.

Validation: 30 unique result IDs match the frozen packet exactly; grouped results and all ten paired score comparisons were calculated from the returned data. The review skill passes the skill validator, and the change passes whitespace checks. The wording changes are narrow; no site source, build output or publication status changed.
