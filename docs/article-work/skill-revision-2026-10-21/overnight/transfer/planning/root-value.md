## Understanding lost if a root section were cut

- **One card, several sets of coordinates:** the reader loses the named-space model and the forward-display/backward-input strategy that makes later operations meaningful.
- **A position and a movement follow different rules:** the reader loses why translation applies to positions but cancels from movement, and why stored geometry need not change during view movement.
- **Turn the group around a known pivot:** the reader must supply rotation arithmetic and pivot construction despite the commission assuming no graphics mathematics.
- **Six numbers describe the whole placement:** the reader loses the bridge from scalar operations to a general affine representation and the browser's coefficient order.
- **Put the conversions together in the right order:** the reader loses composition reasoning and a concrete diagnosis of zoom-dependent pan caused by swapped order.
- **Follow the pointer back to the card**, including **Connect the maths to browser coordinates:** the reader loses inverse construction, the local hit test, or the essential conversion from CSS client coordinates through the resized SVG.
- **When the way back disappears:** the reader loses the explanation of information loss and the distinction between an impossible inverse and an existing but error-amplifying one.
- **Keep the chosen point under the pointer:** the root ceases to complete its promised implementation story; the reader must invent how stored parameters change during zoom and drag before following the deeper page.

The proposed comparisons earn their attention through different decisions: point versus movement, pivot choice, operation order, browser resizing and collapse. Their shared state avoids the cost of learning a new example for each. No cut or merge is proposed, so there is no claimed opening/reading saving to reconcile against lost understanding.
