name: staycurrent-continuity
description: "Keep what a reader carries through a collection consistent: the shared running example, and every term explained before it is used. Run the pass before returning any draft or repair."
---

Keep one collection example record beside its tree at `docs/coverage/<collection>-example.md`: the shared scenario, entities/roles, starting state, values and notation needed by the collection, with article-specific extensions and their correspondence to the core example. The orchestrator settles it with the root breakdown before child planning and gives it to every planner/writer. Briefs use that record. Propose and follow missing extensions for central integration; a standalone writer extends the record directly and reports it.

Figures use the recorded scenario and state. Shared example data across articles live in one module imported by their model files.

Before returning every draft or repair, audit the complete reading order, including captions, controls and step text:

1. Locate every technical term's first use and its explanation. Fix unexplained or late-explained terms by explaining at first use, moving use or using plain words. A previously taught term needs a reminder/link and an explicit statement that this article builds on that earlier article.
2. Keep one name per term, entity and option, with control labels matching prose.
3. Check entities, state, values and notation against the example record, and outcomes against earlier figures. For relational examples, include tables, keys and rows.

Maintain one compact continuity record per article in its work record, linked to the example record: first-use decisions and fixes, dependencies on prior teaching, and the audit result. A transcript of every correct first use is unnecessary. On repair, repeat the complete audit and record decisions and changes without duplicating unchanged entries. When definitions, names or examples move/change, check downstream uses and affected articles/figures; send cross-article example changes to the orchestrator.
