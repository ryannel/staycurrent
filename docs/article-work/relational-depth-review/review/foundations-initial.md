# Foundation articles: independent informed review

Reviewed 15 October 2026. This is an informed technical and editorial review, not the collection's uncued first reading. I read the fourteen pages below in source, including their SQL and optional prose; the introductory and foundation sandbox lessons; the complete checkout-attempt helper displayed by the transaction article; and the control-row and checkout-recovery figures. I did not read the fresh-reader log or earlier author/review notes. I compared the explanatory method with the rebuilt PostgreSQL row-versions and query-execution chapters. I have not claimed a rendered accessibility check or executed the examples in this review.

## Overall judgement

These foundations are substantially stronger than the old PostgreSQL branch was. Most already derive consequences from concrete records, expose the caller's decisions, carry examples through failure, and distinguish illustrative models from execution. Matching the PostgreSQL standard should preserve that work. Repeating engine internals here or adding sections simply to lengthen the pages would weaken the collection's division of responsibility.

I found no consequential factual error in the foundation argument. The most useful depth improvement is to make the isolation article's central multi-connection comparison reproducible all the way through rejection and retry, with actual server evidence. The next useful improvements concern practical coverage of index order and join strategies, and onward links into the newly available PostgreSQL chapters. These are bounded additions to already substantial articles.

## Priorities

1. **P2 — Make the isolation comparison executable through its ending.** The page supplies the Repeatable Read write-skew statement and a valid prose overlap, then substitutes Serializable and explains retry. It does not supply one numbered two-connection schedule spanning reset, both attempts, rejection cleanup, and the new decision. The reader must assemble those operational pieces themselves. Add a compact optional server exercise for both levels, an explicit reset of both flags between runs, and the retried statement with its zero-row result. Run it on the isolated PostgreSQL server. An unchanged control row at Repeatable Read does not refresh a snapshot; the existing figure correctly says this, so retain that qualification and show the subsequent Read Committed read when demonstrating the alternative. The browser remains one connection and must not claim to reproduce the overlap.
2. **P2, practical depth — Offer the central index-order investigation in the sandbox.** The index article already has a complete downloadable 60,000-order investigation. Its linked browser lesson only compares a customer lookup with a broad scan. It does not let the reader try the article's most useful reasoning: customer-only versus customer/time/ID order, early stop, a remaining status filter, and covering visibility. A small independent lesson that preserves the returned IDs while exposing changed work would earn its place. Existing PostgreSQL executor-demand and visibility-map lessons can supply onward practice; do not duplicate those lessons merely to increase the count.
3. **P3 — Connect foundations to the new implementation depth.** General query execution, indexes and storage currently send the reader mainly around the foundation branch or out to manuals. Add precise onward links: prepared-plan lifecycle in PostgreSQL/query-execution; access methods in PostgreSQL/indexes; HOT and retained versions in PostgreSQL/row-versions; physical redo/checkpoints in PostgreSQL/recovery. The foundation explanations should remain sufficient for their own promised outcomes.
4. **P3, optional practice — Match the join-algorithm article's browser lesson to its central comparison.** `query-plans` is a good introduction to scan/filter/aggregate counts, but has no join. A narrow/broad join comparison with unchanged answer semantics and `rows × loops` would make the deeper article's browser invitation more useful. Its downloadable setup and experiments already provide this, so this is not a missing technical argument and can be lower priority than genuinely shallow operations articles elsewhere in the collection.

## Technical verification and remaining uncertainty

Primary documentation checked on 15 October 2026:

- [PostgreSQL 18 transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/transaction-iso.html): ordinary statement views, own writes, Read Committed rechecks, Repeatable Read changed-row failures, and Serializable outcomes. The articles' important distinctions agree. The control-lock qualification is particularly worth preserving.
- [PostgreSQL 18 uniqueness checks](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/index-unique-checks.html): a duplicate inserter can wait for the conflicting transaction, then recheck. This supports both the SKU example and the operation-identity recovery mechanism.
- [PostgreSQL 18 DECLARE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-declare.html): ordinary cursor lifetime belongs to the transaction/connection; the fixed-export explanation does not confuse this with a value token.

The displayed complete checkout helper has coherent connection cleanup: rollback ends a known failed attempt, failed cleanup discards the client, and an unconfirmed post-COMMIT result is marked unknown. Its injected rejection classifier is explicitly an application contract. It is not a complete deployable payment system and does not claim to be one.

I read all twelve introductory/foundation lessons (four introductory and eight foundation lessons), including setup, expected errors, transaction boundaries and variation instructions. They are generally excellent small lessons. They correctly label single-connection limits and use distinct example identities where they simplify the article fixture. The practical gaps above concern what they choose to exercise, not misleading execution claims.

This review did not execute multi-session schedules or inspect every rendered figure. The proposed isolation addition should change that evidence specifically. A source review cannot establish mobile figure legibility or keyboard behaviour; retain the coordinator's browser review for those questions.

## Writing judgement

The strongest passages let the evidence do the work: the two prices in Modelling, Ben versus Cara in Querying your data, rows-per-loop in query execution, and the driver's empty `rows` array versus a thrown constraint error. These are concrete enough to transfer to a new problem. The later qualifications generally change a plausible interpretation rather than merely adding caution.

There is some repeated framing across the collection—fresh practice setup invitations, explicit “not a benchmark” boundaries, and conclusions that tell the reader to choose the promise before the mechanism. In individual articles the statements are useful; repeated expansion would make that texture heavier. Keep new material close to the experiment it enables. Do not append a generic checklist to every retained article.

The checkout tour is intentionally less detailed than the main articles and explicitly says so. Its compactness is not evidence of shallow teaching. Conversely, a long article is not automatically complete: the isolation exercise still asks the reader to reconstruct the most important operational ending. Repair that ending rather than expanding its already clear definition of snapshots.

**Handoff:** retain the foundation explanations, commission the bounded isolation practical repair and index-order lesson if capacity permits, add precise onward links, and re-evaluate against the independent sequential reading. No article files were edited during this initial review.
