# First reading of the table aid

I inspected only the new table-light.html and table-dark.html browser pages. I did not read the article, brief, source, or other reviews. The earlier aid review remains unchanged. The in-app browser was unavailable, so I used the available Chrome browser without changing its viewport.

The aid conveys the basic shape of a table: each row gathers information about one product, and each column gives the same kind of information for every product. The introductory sentence says this directly, and the two rows make it easy to verify. I can read across to learn about a product or down to compare names, prices, and stock quantities.

The first row describes product P7, named Blue mug, priced at €20, with five in stock. The second describes P8, named Bowl, priced at €24, with three in stock. I read P7 and P8 as product identifiers rather than quantities or row numbers because the heading is explicit. I understand “one product” here to mean a product type: five blue mugs share one row, rather than each physical mug having its own row.

“Price” naturally reads as the price of one item, although the visible label does not explicitly say “each” or “per item.” That is a small unstated assumption, not an obstacle to understanding rows and columns. I would not infer taxes, currency conversion, or the value of the whole stock from this display. The accessibility table description calls these current prices and available quantities; the visible heading is simply “Price.”

I can see that each displayed ID differs, but the aid alone does not teach that identifiers must be unique, explain what enforces a column's type, or show how this table connects to another table. It also does not establish any physical arrangement on disk. Those are natural limits for an example whose immediate job is to explain rows and columns.

Nothing looks clickable or editable. “Example table” accurately signals a static display. The column headings have no sorting indicators, and the values have no field borders or button treatment. The compact table leaves some unused space in its panel, but the alignment is clear. Both themes keep the text and horizontal rules legible, and the entire aid fits in the inspected viewport.

The example is understandable without surrounding prose. I found no ambiguity that blocks its basic lesson.
