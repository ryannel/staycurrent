# Independent table reading — reader A

Recorded before reading the revised article, its brief, or an account of the aid's intended meaning. I inspected only table-light.html and table-dark.html in the browser, with its existing viewport.

The aid shows a small product catalogue as a table. A row contains the facts about a single product; the column headings tell me what kind of fact each value represents. P7 is the blue mug, its price is €20, and five are in stock. P8 is the bowl, priced at €24, with three in stock. The setup states the row/column distinction directly, and the visible table makes that distinction concrete.

Nothing appears clickable or editable. The “Example table” label accurately describes what is present. I need no surrounding text to read the values or understand the row/column convention. The aid does not itself explain what makes this a database table rather than a spreadsheet, how IDs are used, or what constraints a database enforces. Those are reasonable jobs for the article.

Both themes are readable. The table occupies only part of the panel width, but its four columns remain easy to associate with their values. No material ambiguity found. The only possible minor ambiguity is that Price does not explicitly say “per item”; ordinary catalogue conventions make that my natural reading. This does not require a change unless the surrounding discussion contrasts unit prices and totals.
