Claim checks, using primary documentation opened on 4 October 2026:

- Lines 3–7: manually traced the illustrative data. Customer 8 matches orders 41 and 43; the other two customers do not match. Both routes preserve the result. [SQLite Query Planning](https://www.sqlite.org/queryplanner.html), §§1.1, 1.3, and 1.4 support full scans, an ordered additional index, retrieving records through references, and continuing through multiple matching entries. The draft explicitly calls its record references simplified and engine-dependent; it does not assert a universal physical address format or that adding this index sorts the full table. SQLite's page is unversioned and displays a last-update date of 22 March 2026.
- Lines 9–11: [PostgreSQL 18 B-Tree Structure](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/btree.html#BTREE-STRUCTURE), §65.1.4.1 supports internal directions to lower levels, leaf references to table rows, and splitting a full page with a parent update. The draft correctly says a page “may” split, leaving room for mechanisms that can avoid a split. It labels these physical details PostgreSQL-specific. The new customer-8 order needs a maintained reference for the later lookup to include order 45; the logical explanation does not depend on one physical tuple per key or record.
- Line 13: [PostgreSQL 18 index introduction](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/indexes-intro.html), the paragraphs beginning “Once an index is created,” “Indexes can also benefit,” and “After an index is created,” support maintenance overhead, conditional choice of an index over a scan, and assistance locating UPDATE targets. No numerical speedup or blanket “all writes are slower” claim appears.
- Line 9: [PostgreSQL 18 Index Types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/indexes-types.html), opening and §§11.2.1–11.2.2 support the distinction between an ordered B-tree example and other indexing algorithms. This article need not survey them to meet its commission.
- Line 15: for the commissioned ordinary additional full index that is actually unused, continued insert maintenance follows from the same synchronization requirement. The conclusion is an inference from the stated workload, not an observed timing result. A1 offers a narrower statement of that premise.
