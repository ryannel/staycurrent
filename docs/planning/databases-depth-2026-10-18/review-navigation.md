# First navigation reading

This is my first reading of `navigation-artifact.md`. I read only that artifact and the supplied blind-review voice reference. These observations concern titles and groupings. I have not read the proposed articles, and a title that hides a destination does not establish that its article lacks the material.

## First impression

The broad shape is useful: I can enter a family, stay with general ideas, or take a named implementation branch. Recovery and live change appear as ordinary engineering work in most families. I did not read the full inventory as a course I would have to finish. The opening note explicitly permits optional branches, and the product names give me a reason to skip branches I do not use.

The family entrances are less consistent. Object storage starts with what the interface gives an application. Filesystems starts with the interfaces themselves. Caches starts with a title matching the family. Relational, document and graph databases start by asking me to choose a database. I would open those choosing pages, but I cannot tell whether they first explain what distinguishes the family. The key-value family starts with a “keyed data contract,” which asks me to understand an abstraction before I have been shown its use. Analytics starts with the question the system must answer; that is an inviting entrance, although its eventual engine-selection page is far from the top of the list.

The largest navigation burden is interpreting terminology before choosing a page: “unknown commit outcomes,” “request identity,” “surviving history,” “maintained results,” and the two global-table abbreviations. Some sound like the answer to a problem I have, but I must supply the connection myself.

## Smallest repairs I would make

1. **Make the introductory promise visible in the selection entrances.** My observation is that RL76, DC53 and GR-A01 are the only apparent family introductions, but their titles promise a choice. If they provide both jobs, rename them **“Relational databases: how they work and when to choose one,” “Document databases: how they work and when to choose one,”** and **“Graph databases: how they work and when to choose one.”** If they do not, point from each family heading to its actual introductory page. This is a navigation request, not a request for three new articles.

2. **Give K01 a concrete family label.** “Choosing a keyed data contract” makes me supply what is being chosen. **“Key-value stores: what a key lets you read and change”** would let me recognise the entrance. Keep a choosing promise only if this page actually helps choose a store. W01 can remain an introduction beneath the wide-column grouping, but **“Wide-column stores: partitions and ordered rows”** would travel better as a standalone link.

3. **Put an engine-choice link at the analytics entrance.** AN46 is clearly named but easy to miss below multiple branches. A top-level “Choosing a system → AN46” link is enough; the article need not move. State whether it includes time-series choices. Similarly, point SR02 to the actual implementation-comparison destination, if there is one. I cannot infer that comparison from “When to add a search service.”

4. **Add a task link for “A write reply never arrived.”** CA-A06 and OB-A05 make that problem visible. RL93 is close; **“Retries when a commit reply is missing”** would remove the translation. DC66/DC68, D04/D07, C10, SR21/SR22/SR32 and GR-A08/GR-A22 each leave me choosing among plausible homes. A one-line family task link to the intended home would repair that ambiguity without broadening every title. For filesystems, explicitly say which interface the destination addresses rather than treating all file and block operations as request/reply protocols.

5. **Name the general recovery and live-change homes where they are currently hidden.** DC61 — “Operating a document database” is my only general document-recovery guess. GR-A09 — “Loading and synchronising a graph” is my general graph-live-change guess. Add “Recovery → [intended ID]” and “Live changes → [intended ID]” links at those family entrances. If no supplied page owns the task, leave that visible as an unassigned destination rather than silently pointing to a product-specific branch.

6. **Expand opaque branch labels before asking me to choose them.** D10 — “MREC global tables.” and D12 — “MRSC global tables.” do not tell me their difference. Expand the abbreviations in the titles. The analytics heading **“F. Continuously maintained results”** and AN33 — “When a maintained SQL system earns another service” suggest a specialised branch, but I cannot confidently identify its product category. Add a plain descriptor or example to the group heading so I can decide whether to enter it. I cannot supply that descriptor reliably from these titles alone.

7. **Make selection scope explicit for storage interfaces.** OB-A15 could be **“Choosing and testing an object storage service”** if that is its scope. For filesystems and block storage, add a selection pointer beside FS-G01 telling me where to choose among interface types; FS-C01 should retain its narrower Linux-filesystem scope. I would not rename FS-C01 to imply it answers the broader decision.

## Optional breadth and apparent sequence

The implementation branches are useful optional breadth. MongoDB, PostgreSQL, CockroachDB, Cassandra, ClickHouse, Redis and S3 are recognisable destinations for readers already using them. Their existence does not make the general route feel compulsory.

Three bits of presentation do suggest more sequence than the opening note intends. The analytics letters A–H read like successive parts; the key-value headings advertise page totals and a lab; “General route” versus “MongoDB route” can sound like two courses to complete. Remove the letters and page totals, and use topic names plus **“MongoDB implementation”** consistently. Keep the opening optional-branch note. These are signals about how to enter, not objections to the amount of reading available.

I could find most practical destinations without reading preceding titles in order. The repairs above chiefly expose jobs already suggested by the inventory and mark where I would otherwise have to supply subject knowledge to make a navigation choice.
