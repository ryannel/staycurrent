# Independent review: analytics, retrieval and graphs

Reviewer scope: analytics and column storage, time series, search and vector retrieval, graph databases including RDF. Audience: an intelligent application engineer new to the subject. This is a review of proposed teaching, not an execution check or an article-readiness judgement.

## First account, before reading the proposals

Recorded before opening any proposal or author ability list. These are the abilities I would expect within each subject's stated practitioner scope; they do not prescribe an article for every item.

### Analytics, column storage and time series

An engineer should recognise the difference between serving individual records and answering questions across many records. They should turn a business question into a result with an explicit grain, time range and meaning. They need to catch double-counting through joins, distinguish events from current state, and handle missing values, distinct counts, approximate answers and changing dimensions where those alter a decision.

They should predict which work a column layout saves: reading selected columns, pruning groups, decoding compressed values and processing batches. They should also see what it fails to save: low-selectivity scans, poorly clustered data, large intermediate results, expensive joins, or individual updates. They should choose sort/partition keys from actual predicates and ingestion patterns, then read a plan and measurements to distinguish unnecessary reads from CPU, memory, network or concurrency pressure. Useful aggregate tables and materialised results require an account of their freshness, correction and rebuild costs.

Operating one selected analytical system means taking data from a source into queryable storage, deciding when it becomes visible, making retries safe, checking completeness, applying corrections and deletions, and recovering from failed ingestion or maintenance. The engineer should understand enough of that system's files/parts, merges or compaction, replicas and query execution to diagnose an accumulating backlog or an unexpectedly slow query. They should know the durability and recovery promise of a successful write, test a backup/restore, and measure a workload that includes writes, reads and maintenance. Familiar SQL is useful but cannot stand in for those operations.

Time-series work additionally needs a model of event time versus arrival time, timestamp precision and zones, duplicate samples, out-of-order or late events, and retention. The engineer must choose labels or dimensions without an uncontrolled cardinality bill, interpret gauges/counters and reset behaviour where metrics are in scope, and explain bucket boundaries, missing buckets, rates and downsampling. A correction that arrives after a roll-up is an ordinary case. They must choose a time-series system or an analytical/relational alternative from the workload rather than from the presence of a timestamp.

The boundary excludes constructing an optimiser or compression codec, exhaustive vendor coverage, full data-platform orchestration, and specialist statistical analysis. It must still teach the minimum data and result semantics needed to trust the operational choices it promises.

### Search and vector retrieval

An engineer should first recognise retrieval as producing a useful candidate set and ordering it, with relevance judged against a task rather than against a database's success response. They should distinguish exact filtering, lexical matching, vector similarity and hybrid retrieval, and know the cases in which each can return plausible but wrong results.

For lexical search they need to trace source text through analysis into index terms and back through query analysis, matching and scoring. Token boundaries, normalisation, stemming, synonyms, phrase positions, field choice and language differences matter when they explain a failed query. They should understand enough of ranking, field length and term frequency to repair a poor result without treating every boost as an independent knob.

For vector retrieval they need to choose the represented unit, prepare chunks and metadata, choose an embedding model and compatible distance measure, and track model/index versions. They must separate representation quality from approximate-index recall, filtering losses and final ranking. They should measure exact versus approximate neighbours on a bounded corpus, and compare useful results against labelled tasks. Hybrid candidate budgets and reranking need an account of which relevant result was lost before the reranker could see it. Updating an embedding model requires backfill, coexistence or cutover and a rollback plan, not merely a different API parameter.

Operating a selected retrieval system includes ingestion, visibility delay, update/delete propagation, mapping/schema changes, reindexing, aliases or equivalent cutover, backup/rebuild and load management. Permissions and tenant filters must survive every retrieval path, including cached candidates and reranking where those paths are taught. A stale index can expose a deleted or forbidden item. The engineer must diagnose empty, irrelevant and slow results using stages and evidence, and decide which data is authoritative when the index can be rebuilt.

The boundary excludes training embedding models, inventing ranking algorithms, an information-retrieval research curriculum, and a general generation/RAG course. It still includes relevance evaluation, freshness and access control because an application search engineer cannot responsibly operate retrieval without them. Selected products need practical operating depth; unselected products need an honest scope statement and enough contrast to support selection.

### Graph databases, property graphs and RDF

An engineer should identify when relationships and path questions make a graph model useful, and when joins or an adjacency table suffice. They should turn one domain into vertices/edges or triples with stable identities and deliberate relationship meaning. Direction, multiplicity, properties, identifiers and lifecycle must survive import, correction and deletion. A graph is not automatically a performant answer to an unbounded traversal.

For a property graph, the engineer should trace a traversal or query through matching, expanding and filtering, predict multiplicative rows and repeated paths, and bound expensive exploration without silently changing the question. Cycles, optional matches, variable-length paths, duplicate results, shortest paths and relationship direction need worked cases. Constraints, indexes, plans, memory and transaction boundaries should explain how to make a query safe under real data and concurrent changes.

For RDF, they need the triple model, IRIs, blank nodes, typed/language-tagged literals and named graphs. They should distinguish shared vocabulary from verified truth, and open-world semantics from closed-world validation. SPARQL needs actual matching and solution behaviour, including joins, OPTIONAL, FILTER, duplicate solutions, property paths and updates where the selected store supports them. They should recognise the consequences of identity assertions, basic entailment and inference settings, and know when validation such as SHACL is needed instead. Provenance, dataset boundaries and named graph access matter when combining sources. This is not interchangeable with a property graph plus a different query syntax.

Operating either selected graph implementation requires repeatable import, identity and duplicate handling, updates/deletes, useful constraints or validation, query diagnosis, concurrency/durability scope, backup/restore and an upgrade/change workflow. A property-graph exercise cannot satisfy an RDF store's practical obligations. Selection should compare the questions and interoperability needs rather than promise a generic winner.

The boundary excludes graph-algorithm research, exhaustive ontology engineering, full logical foundations, and every graph product. Specialist reasoning and federation may be scoped or deferred, but their presence and failure modes must be named if they materially change ordinary query interpretation or the selected implementation's promise.

## Proposal findings

First findings recorded after reading the three assigned proposals and consulting the relevant ability rows. I did not read old plans, inventories, other reviews or integration decisions. I also read the collection/article and product-boundary rules in `docs/content-structure.md`, without following its planning links. This is a practitioner/editorial reading of cards and journeys, not a sequential comprehension test of finished prose.

### Concrete repairs

**RA1 — The time-series route does not yet earn its selection decision.**

- **Outcome at stake:** choose whether a measurement workload needs a specialist store, ordinary relational storage, an analytical store, or a monitoring system, then shortlist a suitable implementation. Otherwise a reader can learn sophisticated rate and retention semantics and still choose Timescale because it is the only operated example.
- **Exact homes checked:** AN23–AN29, AN46's decision/boundary, ANU47, ANU64, and the time-series paragraph under “Hard boundaries and unresolved decisions”. AN46 sends time-series-specific decisions to AN23–AN29. AN23 establishes measurement/monitoring boundaries; AN26 establishes physical design; AN27–AN29 operate Timescale. None owns a worked implementation choice. The boundary says InfluxDB, QuestDB and Prometheus storage/operation may enter a future comparison if a workload makes them credible alternatives.
- **Why owed:** the ability artifact's finish line explicitly includes choosing whether time-series specialisation deserves investment, and the proposal gives this subject a direct entrance and full selected implementation. It cannot discharge selection merely by postponing every competing time-series implementation. This does not require all those named vendors or another operating branch.
- **Smallest repair:** add a bounded time-series selection case to AN46, reachable directly from AN23/AN26. Use the same workload contract to compare Timescale with an existing relational solution and one credible specialised or analytical alternative. Include series cardinality, late corrections, historical joins, retention/rollups, operating responsibility and the trial evidence that decides. AN23's monitoring contrast can remain a local boundary. AN46 already owns engine selection; a new article is unnecessary unless the worked case proves too large.

**RA2 — Several analytical entrances silently require SQL beyond this audience.**

- **Outcome at stake:** a newcomer with programming and record knowledge can reach a first correct analytical answer without an unannounced prior SQL course.
- **Exact homes checked:** AN01 entry (“basic records and SQL”), AN02 entry (“understand a join”), AN03 entry (“grouped SQL”), AN44 entry (“basic SQL”), and the boundary assigning SQL grouping/window mechanics a concise reminder or provisional relational link.
- **Why owed:** the commission gives readers basic programming and records. The proposal's first entrance and immediate local-analysis route raise that entry bar. I supplied the meanings of projection, joins, groups and SQL result multiplicity from my own knowledge; the plan only explicitly promises some of those explanations locally. An uncommissioned relational link cannot be the necessary first lesson.
- **Smallest repair:** make AN01 records-only and require the first query examples in AN02/AN03 and AN44 to show input records, the short request and resulting records together. Teach the few SELECT/filter/group/join operations the case needs beside it; keep broader SQL teaching external. State this local support in the cards instead of listing basic SQL as an assumed entrance. This is small teaching within existing homes, not another introductory article.

**RA3 — RDF equality is in the ability list but lacks an explicit worked consequence.**

- **Outcome at stake:** combine sources without treating two names as automatically different entities, or treating an equality claim as a harmless resemblance link. Mistaken identity can contaminate answers beyond the imported assertion itself.
- **Exact homes checked:** GR-A15's IRI/blank-node/source-graph cases; GR-A16's domain inference, validation, deletion and vocabulary-change cases; GR35's “vocabulary entailment and equality”. GR-A15 treats accidental identity and GR-A16 treats type entailment, but neither card identifies a changed equality/identity case. The ability exists; the proposed teaching is still implicit.
- **Why owed:** this collection promises RDF identity and combining assertions. Full ontology engineering can remain outside that promise, but ordinary source integration needs the consequences of an identity assertion and the selected entailment regime's limits.
- **Smallest repair:** add one two-source identity case to GR-A16, linked from GR-A15: distinguish keeping two IRIs, asserting supported equality, and recording a weaker correspondence; show how the chosen interpretation changes query answers. If equality reasoning lies outside the chosen reasoner fragment, say so and preserve the mapping as data rather than silently merging entities. No OWL catalogue or extra page is needed.

### Boundaries to tighten before briefs

**RA4 — AN42 carries three independently substantial operating jobs.** Its worksheet sets tenant/access/resource policy, diagnoses merge/replica/disk debt, and budgets a layout/version change. The first two connect through the same tenant service budget. A version upgrade introduces compatibility, sequence, rollback and recovery evidence that the overload case does not establish. The consequence is an apparent practical upgrade lesson that ends at a planning checklist. AN15 owns general migration, AN38 already owns actual schema/physical correction, and AN43 owns actual recovery. Tighten those cross-links and specify whether AN42 applies an upgrade procedure or merely establishes the change window. If safe product upgrade execution remains a finish-line claim, give it a named investigation with its own changed failure case; that may be an independently entered section or a page after the actual worksheet is designed. Do not add a page just because this card is long, but do not leave its present “split if substantial” condition as the only commissioning decision.

**RA5 — SR09 needs an honest boundary between a first vector model and specialised representations.** The page combines distance/normalisation, an embedding/version manifest, learned sparse retrieval and token-vector late interaction. These are useful distinctions, but a newcomer arriving at “What similarity vectors preserve” could pay for three access/scoring models before reaching their first exact-neighbour reference in SR10. The proposed bounded sparse and late-interaction traces are preferable to omitting them. Keep dense similarity/compatibility as the core route, make the two contrasting representation traces explicitly optional selection-level branches, and name their different scoring/storage consequences. A broader title such as “Representations for retrieval” would make that promise clearer. Do not create a specialist serving branch or claim one is taught by these cases. This is an entrance/reading-cost repair, not a missing whole ability chain.

**RA6 — GR-A10 should explicitly apply, rather than re-own, weighted-route teaching.** GR-A05 already owns shortest-by-hops/weights, eligibility and algorithm assumptions under GR12. GR-A10 includes weighted routes again under GR25 “in full”, beside reachability/components and centrality/communities. The smallest repair is to mark weighted routes as a short reminder/application of GR-A05 and choose the particular component and centrality/community outputs the package decision needs. The page's useful job is interpreting analytical results; it should not become several algorithm introductions. I would retain GR-A10/11 as separate pages: interpreting a high rank and managing a stale, memory-heavy projection are different failures.

### What should survive revision

The analytical plan goes beyond fast scans in the right places. AN02/03 make an incorrect total a separate problem from a slow query. AN10 explicitly tests source transactions split across analytical tables; AN12 requires semantic reconciliation rather than a finished progress bar. AN25 and AN28 retain the final consequence of late corrections after raw history expires. AN37/38/40 separately teach replay identity, direct correction and derived-target maintenance: merging those into a single “updates” article would hide different ways to return a wrong answer. AN17–AN22 correctly treat a table as a catalogue/metadata/object/engine system and give its selected stack real recovery work. DuckDB's small branch is proportionate to its explicitly local promise; it need not mirror ClickHouse's cluster branch.

The search proposal is the strongest complete ability chain of the three as written. Its ordinary lexical route reaches analysis, matching, scoring and judged usefulness without requiring ANN. SR18/20 separate useful answers from exact-neighbour agreement and from load measurements. SR13 retains the filter during recall repair; SR24 carries access through non-hit endpoints, rerankers, caches and hydration. SR23 preserves deletion through backfill, rollback and restore. Those are substantial operational obligations, not reassurance paragraphs. I found no missing whole search ability chain against the first account. SR17's fetch and paging cases cohere around the identity/version of one displayed hit; I would not split them simply because there are two mechanisms. SR07/08, SR10–12 and SR16/17 have distinct sought problems and do not earn a merge on count alone.

The graph plan earns its RDF branch. GR-A15–17 teach different answer semantics, not a second syntax for the same property graph. GR-A28–32 supply storage, transactions, configured inference/validation, endpoint safety and recovery; shrinking that to an RDF overview would leave the stated operating promise false. GR-A08's concurrent cycle check and GR-A22's driver retry make application invariants concrete. GR-A23/25 distinguish restart from a useful restore. GR-A30 makes configured inference a deployed view with a lifecycle, rather than a capability inferred from a product name. GR-A24's standard-cluster exercise and separately deferred property-sharding operation are an honest scope distinction.

### Shared teaching and scope disagreements

The plans repeat the broad source → projection → replay → reconciliation structure in AN10/12, SR21/23 and GR-A09. I do **not** recommend deleting those homes solely because that diagram recurs. Analytical cross-table publication, permission/embedding generations, and graph endpoint/assertion identity supply different final consequences. A later shared-home decision should extract only the identical core and retain those worked applications and sufficient local reminders. A planned shared systems programme cannot be used as the missing explanation.

I disagree with making warehouse, vector-service, distributed-graph or monitoring vendor symmetry a completeness criterion. ClickHouse does not teach operating BigQuery; Elasticsearch does not teach Qdrant; TDB2 does not teach a clustered RDF service. The proposals say this plainly. Their selection comparisons need consequential alternatives; their unselected implementations do not need equal page allocations. Likewise, Materialize operation, general stream processing, ontology design, model training and graph ML can remain deferred under the stated finish lines.

The open edition/licence/environment questions are material commissioning gates, especially Elasticsearch access/inference, Neo4j Enterprise/GDS, the Iceberg catalogue and Timescale's selected facilities. The cards already disclose them. They are not evidence that the practitioner outcome has been achieved, nor a reason to substitute a browser model for native recovery or permission tests. Product choices may change after user discussion without invalidating the general ability chains.

### Judgement and limits

**Ready for user discussion, with concrete repair required before article commissions.** RA1–RA3 should become explicit teaching commitments; RA4 needs a bounded implementation promise before its brief. RA5/RA6 can be resolved in the affected cards without expanding the tree. The strongest disagreements concern allocation and entrance cost, not wholesale missing coverage. This review can establish that the planned outcomes and boundaries are credible after those repairs. It cannot establish that a future page teaches them, that an environment is reproducible, that claims are factually verified, or that anything is ready to publish.
