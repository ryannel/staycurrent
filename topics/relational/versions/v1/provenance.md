## Synthesis

- The stance: Postgres first, MySQL where a team runs it well or needs Vitess today, is my judgement from the mechanisms above and the 2026 vendor consolidation around Postgres.
- "Tens of thousands of simple transactions per second on one large NVMe-backed server" is my working figure, not a documented one; no primary source publishes node throughput.
- The capacity worked example (150-byte rows, 54 per page, 20 bytes per B-tree entry, 90 per cent leaf fill, 25 per cent bloat headroom, IOPS from misses plus WAL) is my arithmetic from the documented page and header sizes. The 8-byte index tuple header is from my reading of the source and was not re-fetched.
- "A B-tree leaf entry is the key plus about 12 bytes" is my approximation of the index tuple header plus line pointer.
- The claim that MySQL's per-process default connection limit is higher than Postgres's, and the InnoDB figures of 1,017 columns and 64 secondary indexes, are from memory of Oracle's manual and are flagged as such in the text.
- MySQL's `sync_binlog`, `gtid_mode`, and `innodb_doublewrite` defaults are not quoted because I could not extract them cleanly from the source in this session.
- The undo-chain slowdown from a long transaction on InnoDB, and the history-list-length monitoring advice, are operational experience.
- The autovacuum tuning advice (scale factor around 0.01, a fixed threshold, a higher cost limit) is practice, not documentation.
- Read-your-writes strategies (pinning a session to the primary, waiting for a replay LSN) are standard practice; only the PostgreSQL 19 command is sourced.
- The statement that almost everyone runs a cross-region standby asynchronously, and that Group Replication multi-primary stays within one datacentre in practice, are judgement.
- The interview answers' recommendations (constraint first for write skew, partition before shard, dedicated shard for a giant tenant, UUIDv7 in `BINARY(16)`) are my recommendations grounded in the documented mechanisms.
- That "most self-managed sites actually run pgBackRest" is an observation of practice, not a measured share.
- The managed cost model's four lines are my summary; no prices are quoted because the pricing pages could not be fetched.
- "In practice MySQL means InnoDB" and "MariaDB diverges a little more every year" are judgement.
- The description of the BDR-descended commercial extensions and Aurora DSQL as resolving conflicts after the fact or rejecting at commit is a summary from the databases topic's research, not re-verified here.
