## Synthesis

- The stance, that these are for data you can rebuild and never the only copy, is my reading of the documented replication and failover guarantees; the documentation supports each mechanism but doesn't state the rule.
- Salvatore Sanfilippo starting Redis in 2009, Redis Ltd employing the core developers since 2015 and holding the trademark, and Azure Cache as a managed offering are from general knowledge, not from a source fetched for this cut.
- "One thread does all the work" as the reason for speed, and the write-path diagram, are my synthesis of the config comments, the latency guide and the persistence and replication documentation.
- The description of the Redis 6 and 7 I/O threads as writing replies by default, with socket reads and parsing handed to them only under `io-threads-do-reads yes`, is from the redis.conf comments on those lines; that the Redis 8 rewrite has the threads read, parse and write is from the 8.0 release notes; the Valkey 8.0 prefetch behaviour is from valkey.conf; the claim that Valkey 9.0 made the count changeable at runtime is from its release notes.
- The LFU counter being 8 bits, quicklist nodes being about 8 KB (`list-max-listpack-size -2`), and transparent huge pages meaning 2 MB copy-on-write pages are from general knowledge of the implementation, consistent with the config comments.
- The claim that expired keys "can linger" until touched or sampled follows from the documented sampling algorithm.
- "Cache mode" versus "store mode" is my naming for the two configurations, not a term the documentation uses.
- The statement that a transaction sees a consistent state "because nothing else runs" is my reading of the single-threaded design, not a documented isolation level.
- ElastiCache Global Datastore as asynchronous cross-region replication with a managed promote, Redis Enterprise active-active being CRDT-based, ECPUs being AWS's unit of request work, Memorystore pricing per node-hour, Redis Cloud pricing by plan size and Upstash billing per command are from prior knowledge; the pricing pages for the last three could not be fetched.
- The 20 per cent Valkey discount on ElastiCache is computed from the price list figures (0.1752 / 0.219), not stated by AWS in a fetched source.
- The capacity worked examples (50 million keys at 200 bytes; 20 million sessions at 600 bytes with fragmentation at 1.3 and a 2x snapshot allowance, about $750 a month for three r7g.xlarge nodes) are my arithmetic on the documented and priced figures, and the 1.2 to 1.5 fragmentation range and the 70 per cent `maxmemory` rule are operating judgement.
- The description of the forkless snapshot as a background thread using the per-key metadata to write from inside the process is inferred from the config comment and release notes; the exact mechanism was not documented in the sources reached.
- The interview answers' recommendations (fencing tokens for correctness locks, two weighted counters for high-limit rate limiting, splitting hot keys into sixteen sub-counters, a one-second local cache and single-flight fetches for a read-hot key, update-then-delete with a jittered TTL and log-driven invalidation for the cached product page, snapshotting from a replica, keeping authoritative scores in the database) are my judgement, grounded in the mechanisms cited above.
- The Caching patterns section (cache-aside, write-through and write-behind, invalidation versus TTL, TTL jitter, negative caching, the four stampede defences, and the orderings of the cache-versus-database race with their failure windows) is written from general knowledge of the patterns, not from a source fetched for this cut; none of it is Redis-specific beyond the commands shown. The lease mechanism, the once-per-ten-seconds lease rate and the mcsqueal daemon are from Nishtala et al., "Scaling Memcache at Facebook" (NSDI 2013), summarised from prior knowledge and not fetched; Debezium reading the Postgres WAL is from general knowledge of that tool.
- The Kleppmann critique is summarised from memory because the post could not be fetched; the Valkey distributed-locks page confirms that it exists and that fencing tokens and clock drift are the points at issue.
- "Redis Cluster supports only database 0" is from the cluster tutorial's statement that cluster mode does not support multiple databases; Valkey 9.0 adding numbered databases in cluster mode is from its release notes.
- The 8.10.2 release "alongside security fixes across every 8.x line" is from the redis/docs release-notes index showing 8.2.9, 8.4.6, 8.6.6 and 8.8.2 in August 2026 with the same CVE, and 8.4.7 and 8.10.2 on 17 September 2026.
- Dragonfly 2.0 "shipped the same day" as Valkey 9.2.0-rc1 (16 September 2026) is from the GitHub release page; one indexed article gives 17 September, which may be a time-zone difference.
