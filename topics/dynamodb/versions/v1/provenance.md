## Synthesis

- The claim that GSI write throttling "backpressures" the base table and that low-cardinality GSI keys create their own hot partitions is documented; the advice to shard the GSI key (`OPEN#<n>`) is the standard practitioner pattern and my recommendation.
- "In practice the window is well under a second" for eventually consistent reads is my judgement from operating the service; the documentation only promises a repeated read "after a short time".
- The partition storage split threshold of about 10 GB is inferred from the documented 10 GB item-collection limit with LSIs and from AWS engineering blogs; the current partitions page doesn't print the number.
- The break-even of roughly 30% utilisation between on-demand and provisioned is my arithmetic: one WCU-hour at $0.00065 buys 3,600 writes, or $0.18 per million, against $0.625 per million on-demand, a ratio of about 3.5; the read ratio is the same ($0.036 against $0.125 per million). On-demand therefore costs the same as provisioned capacity used 29% of the time.
- The sessions-store sizing and prices are my worked example using the documented unit rules and listed prices; "half that again with reserved capacity" applies the advertised 54% one-year discount.
- The order-system key design and the "replicate a hot item under N keys" fix are my recommendations, built from the documented per-partition ceiling and transaction semantics.
- The statement that MRSC is "one of the few" managed strongly consistent multi-region stores is my framing; Spanner and CockroachDB offer comparable guarantees with different models.
- The reading of the October 2025 event ("a regional endpoint is a single dependency however replicated the storage is") is my interpretation of the AWS post-event summary.
- Interview answers throughout are my view of what a strong candidate would say, grounded in the sourced mechanisms above.
