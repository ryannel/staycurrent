## Synthesis

- Spanner's split-by-load behaviour, the bit-reversed sequence and UUID advice, and `STORING` and `NULL_FILTERED` are from general knowledge of the Google Cloud documentation, which the proxy blocked. The 65%/45% CPU targets, the 7-day retention maximum, and the 10 TiB per-node ceiling were confirmed from indexed search results during the editorial pass and are stated in the article without a hedge.
- The two-region write-latency estimate in the interview section applies the paper's commit-wait and Paxos figures to a round-trip time I estimated for Iowa to South Carolina; it is a worked estimate, not a measurement.
- The global inventory design and the Spanner hotspot answer apply the documented key-ordering and quorum rules; the specific choices are judgement.
- The statement that Spanner's client libraries retry `ABORTED` transactions for you is from general knowledge of the client libraries.
- Cockroach Labs' founding in New York in 2015 by three former Google engineers, and the move to the Business Source License in 2019, are from general knowledge rather than a fetched page.
- The deprecation of interleaved tables in v20.2 is from memory of the release notes; the removal in v21.2 is supported only by the grammar comparison above.
- The read-path description of an intent encounter (check the record, queue if pending) combines the transaction layer's conflict and TxnWaitQueue sections into one narrative.
- The write-path diagram and numbered steps combine the transaction, distribution, and replication layer pages and the life-of-a-transaction page into a single sequence; the ordering of latch, lock table, and timestamp cache checks follows those pages.
- The claim that a secondary index write is a distributed transaction across as many ranges as there are indexes follows from the key encoding and range model; the docs state the cost more generally as "new values have to be written for both the table and the index".
- The capacity worked example (6 nodes of 16 vCPUs for 2 TiB and 30,000 QPS) applies the documented per-vCPU ratios and the 4-active-connections-per-vCPU rule (30,000 QPS at a few milliseconds is about 100 concurrent executions, so about 25 vCPUs at the floor, doubled); it's a first cut, not a documented result.
