# Distributed SQL: Spanner and CockroachDB — Changelog

## v1 — 2026-09-29

The founding cut. This piece profiles the distributed SQL family through its two flagships, Spanner and CockroachDB, as they stand in September 2026, at the depth a Staff+ engineer needs for a system-design deep dive.

Each section opens with the mechanism the family shares and then shows how each system builds it: a stateless SQL layer over a sorted key space cut into ranges or splits, one consensus group per range with a leader that serves reads, and timestamps that make the store multi-versioned. For Spanner it covers the 2012 OSDI and 2017 SIGMOD papers, spanservers and tablets on Colossus, Paxos groups with 10-second leader leases, read-write, read-only, and witness replicas across regional, dual-region, and multi-region configurations, TrueTime with its 1 to 7 ms uncertainty and the commit-wait that yields external consistency, two-phase locking and two-phase commit across splits, the serialisable default and the newer repeatable read option, interleaved tables and bit-reversed sequences, the 80,000 mutation-mod limit, GoogleSQL and the PostgreSQL dialect through PGAdapter, nodes and processing units, the five-nines SLA, backups, version retention, change streams, Data Boost, editions pricing, and Spanner Graph and vector search. For CockroachDB it covers ranges of 512 MiB with a Raft group each, leader leases, Pebble with MVCC and a four-hour garbage collection window, hybrid logical clocks with a 500 ms offset bound, write intents, parallel commits, and buffered writes, the client retry loop that serialisable isolation demands and read committed as the trade, hotspots and hash-sharded indexes, survival goals and table localities, node sizing, backups and rolling upgrades, the November 2024 licence change, and Cloud pricing.

YugabyteDB, TiDB, and Aurora DSQL each get a paragraph where they differ, and fourteen interview questions close it out, including a Spanner hotspot, a two-region write-latency estimate, and a global inventory design.
