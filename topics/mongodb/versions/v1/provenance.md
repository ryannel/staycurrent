## Synthesis

- The stance, that MongoDB fits when one read should return one entity and that its strongest guarantees need to be asked for, is my reading of the mechanisms, in particular the `local` default read concern against the `majority` default write concern.
- "Nobody pools in front of MongoDB the way they do with Postgres" and the one-megabyte-per-connection figure are practitioner rules of thumb, not documented limits.
- The 1,024-byte index key limit being removed in 4.2 is from my own knowledge of the 4.2 release; the current limits page simply no longer lists it.
- The description of the write path ordering (page update and oplog append in one storage transaction, then journal, then checkpoint) combines the WiredTiger and journaling pages with the replication README; the manual doesn't narrate it as one sequence.
- The capacity rule of "RAM about twice the working set plus 1 GB", the "third to a half" compression ratio for snappy, and the 20 per cent resharding headroom are my sizing conventions, derived from the cache formula and typical compression, not vendor guidance.
- The statement that sub-millisecond single-document reads from cache are typical is experience, not a documented figure; MongoDB publishes no absolute throughput numbers.
- Which cloud provider front ends exist and why (the SSPL) is my interpretation of the sequence of events; the vendors haven't said so.
- The interview answers' recommendations (compound tenant-plus-time keys, hashed keys for monotonic inserts, reserving transactions for cross-document changes, and the not-use cases) are my judgement grounded in the documented mechanisms.
- Treating the config shard as an 8.0 feature follows the 8.0 release notes; the underlying capability may have existed in 7.0 as a preview.
- The claim that most teams run on Atlas in 2026 is an impression from the market, not a measured share.
