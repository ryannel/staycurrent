## Synthesis

- The framing that Elasticsearch and OpenSearch share every mechanism described here, differing in licence, governance, and feature set, is my judgement from the fork history; OpenSearch was forked after the 7.0 coordination rewrite, so the quorum, in-sync set, translog, and refresh mechanisms are the same code lineage.
- That Lucene's term dictionary is a finite-state transducer, that numeric fields use a block k-d tree, and that deletes are recorded in a per-segment live-documents bitset are standard Lucene internals from my own knowledge, not fetched from Lucene's file-format documentation, which was blocked.
- Doc values arriving in Lucene 4.0 in 2012 is from Elastic's "Elasticsearch as a column store" blog as indexed, and my recollection.
- The claim that the pre-7.0 `minimum_master_nodes` setting defaulted to 1 and so allowed split brain when left unset is from my own knowledge of Zen discovery; the removal of the setting in 7.0 is sourced.
- The 1% of heap default for the shard request cache (`indices.requests.cache.size`) is from my recollection; the fetched page did not state the figure.
- The 2 MB floor segment and ten segments per tier are from an Elastic discussion of Lucene's tiered merge policy as indexed, not from a fetched reference page.
- MurmurHash3 as the routing hash is from Elastic's shard-routing blog as indexed.
- Cross-cluster replication being a paid (Platinum-level) Elasticsearch feature is from my knowledge of Elastic's subscription matrix; the fetched documentation page did not state the licence.
- That OpenSearch renamed the master role to cluster manager is from my knowledge of the 2.x renaming.
- That Elastic Cloud Hosted bills per hour by node size and subscription tier is my summary of its pricing page as indexed; no specific figure is quoted.
- The Postgres full-text comparison (`tsvector` with a GIN index, `ts_rank` not being BM25, ParadeDB `pg_search` adding BM25) reuses the overview research notes for this site.
- The "half of RAM to page cache, so a node's ceiling is how much hot index fits there" reasoning, the capacity worked examples, and the "practical ceiling is the cluster state" statement are my judgement, built from the sourced limits.
- Every interview answer is my judgement about what a strong candidate would say, grounded in the sourced mechanisms; the specific shard counts and node counts in the worked examples are arithmetic on the sourced guidance, not vendor recommendations.
- The years attached to the GitHub release tag dates (9.1.0 in 2025, 9.2.0 in 2025, 9.3.0 in 2026, 9.4.0 in 2026; OpenSearch 3.2.0 and 3.3.0 in 2025) are inferred from release order, because the tag pages show only day and month.
