## Synthesis

- The stance, that ClickHouse is the engine for sub-second aggregation over append-only rows and pays for it with slow point lookups, expensive updates and memory-bound joins, is my judgement from the mechanisms above rather than a claim any one source makes.
- "Roughly $0.30 per unit-hour" is derived from the docs' $436.95 example for two 8 GiB replicas over a month; the docs don't print the rate.
- The capacity worked example (5 billion events a day, 300 bytes each, a 5x compression ratio, a 3x peak) uses assumed inputs; the ratios applied to them are the docs' sizing guidance. Sizing the Cloud variant on a seven-day hot window with the customer-facing 1:30 ratio, rather than on the full retention at 1:100, is my judgement about how object storage plus a local cache changes what RAM is for; the docs' ratios are written for local disks.
- The events pipeline design, the choice of `(tenant_id, event_type, toStartOfHour(event_time), user_id)` as the sorting key, the hot-tenant remedies, and the recommended fixes in each interview answer are practitioner judgement grounded in the documented mechanisms.
- "Usually well under a second" for replication lag in normal operation is an observation, not a documented figure.
- The statement that the practical ceiling on a self-managed shard is the biggest disk you can attach is inference from the VLDB paper's "several dozen terabytes" remark and the sizing guide's advice to buy the largest server.
- The comparison with Snowflake and BigQuery (where each wins) and the DuckDB threshold of "a few hundred gigabytes on one machine" are judgement calls, not vendor claims.
- The summary that ClickHouse keeps serving reads and refuses inserts on any replica that can't reach Keeper during a partition is my reading of the documented behaviour under Keeper loss and asynchronous replication.
- The description of how `ON CLUSTER` DDL moves through a Keeper queue and how the Replicated database engine replays DDL from its own log is written from working knowledge of the engine; the timeouts, defaults and the Cloud-only defaults quoted beside it come from the settings source above. That Cloud runs on the Replicated database engine is inferred from those Cloud defaults rather than stated on a page I read on this date.
- The `vector_similarity` index first appearing experimentally in 24.8 is from the 2024 release notes as I know them and was not re-verified on this date; the beta (25.5) and GA (25.8) dates were.
